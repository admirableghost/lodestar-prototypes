# 1. Micromutual

> *Neighbours contribute to a shared pool funding joint prevention work and defined smaller losses, alongside (not replacing) existing home insurance.*

## 1.1 Who is already doing this

**The closest live institutional work — Community-Based Catastrophe Insurance (CBCI).** A framework developed by the Wharton Risk Management and Decision Processes Center with Marsh McLennan, published February 2021, authored by **Alex Bernhardt, Carolyn Kousky, Andy Read and Christopher Sykes** ([Marsh CBCI report](https://www.corporate.marsh.com/assets/insights/publications/2021/february/Community--Based--Catastrophe--Insurance--(Final).pdf)). CBCI is a disaster insurance programme arranged by a local government, quasi-governmental body such as a special-purpose district, or community group, covering individual properties within the community ([Guy Carpenter](https://www.guycarp.com/insights/community-based-catastrophe-insurance.html)). Guy Carpenter sets out **four delivery models — facilitator, group policy, aggregator, and community captive** — requiring increasing levels of community commitment (same source).

**How it differs from Micromutual:** CBCI is nearly always *a community entity buying insurance from a licensed carrier on residents' behalf*, not residents mutualising risk among themselves. That is the single most important structural difference in this section. The Guy Carpenter material explicitly notes that providing full homeowners cover through these models is much harder because it requires ongoing claims administration across many perils, larger coverage levels, more modelling and "greater regulatory scrutiny" ([Guy Carpenter / Kousky](https://www.guycarp.com/insights/community-based-catastrophe-insurance.html)).

**Live and named CBCI pilots:**

| Programme | What it is | Status / geography |
|---|---|---|
| **Isleton, California** (Delta Geologic Hazard Abatement District) | First community-based parametric flood product arranged by a California special district; supplements, does not replace, existing cover; parametric payouts when floodwater reaches set levels; residents may use payments for repairs or evacuation costs | Launched **October 2024**, **$200,000** two-year grant from CA Dept of Water Resources, split evenly across years ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/catastrophe/california-launches-firstever-communitybased-flood-program-510367.aspx)) |
| **Center for NYC Neighborhoods flood pilot** | Nonprofit buys a parametric policy; pays cash grants to low/moderate-income households after qualifying rainfall. Swiss Re capacity, Guy Carpenter broking, ICEYE data | Launched **February 2023**, 12-month trial, up to **$1.1m** of cover, up to **$15,000** per household ([Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/swiss-re-guy-carpenter-iceye-deliver-nyc-parametric-flood-insurance/); [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/east/2023/03/08/711441.htm)). **The policy had not been triggered** at the time of EDF's own lessons-learned write-up ([EDF Market Forces, Nov 2023](https://blogs.edf.org/markets/2023/11/02/harnessing-community-insurance-lessons-from-nycs-innovative-post-flood-assistance-program/)) |
| **Franklin / St Mary Parish, Louisiana** | Parametric flood for a rural underserved region, with Louisiana CPRA, Guy Carpenter, City of Franklin, St Mary Community Action Agency, LA Dept of Insurance | Pilot; **payouts around $2,000** per household ([The Water Institute](https://thewaterinstitute.org/franklin-cbci-pilot)) |
| **Mississippi River Cities and Towns Initiative** | Flood parametric pilot in development with Munich Re | In development ([search summary via MRCTI/Guy Carpenter materials](https://www.guycarp.com/insights/community-based-catastrophe-insurance.html)) |
| **Guy Carpenter "community-based parametric reciprocal exchange" research** | Research into whether groups of homeowners/small businesses can pool nat-cat risk in a reciprocal exchange, for **California and Florida**, with FIU Institute of Environment, Climate Adaptation Center Sarasota, UC Santa Cruz and University of Michigan | Announced **13 December 2024**; explicitly researching the *regulatory environment* to lay foundations for future pilots — i.e. no pilot yet ([Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/guy-carpenter-launches-new-parametric-solutions-research-project-for-california-florida/)) |

**The prevention half of Micromutual is already an insurance-regulated mechanism, and it is community-scoped.** California's **Safer from Wildfires** regulation (10 CCR §2644.9), effective **14 October 2022**, requires every admitted residential property insurer that uses wildfire risk in pricing to file discounts for listed mitigation measures, and the menu includes a **community** category: participation in **Firewise USA** or being in a **Fire Risk Reduction Community** certified by the CA Board of Forestry ([CDI Safer from Wildfires](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/200-wrr/Safer-from-Wildfires.cfm); [NFPA](https://www.nfpa.org/news-blogs-and-articles/blogs/2022/12/15/new-rules-benefit-california-property-owners-in-firewise-sites)).

**IBHS has now standardised the exact cross-boundary problem the pamphlet's page 4 describes.** On **9 June 2026** IBHS added **Wildfire Prepared Neighborhood** and **Wildfire Prepared Multifamily** standards, with Essential and Enhanced designation levels, specifically to address "structure-to-structure fire spread" beyond individual homes ([IBHS news release](https://ibhs.org/ibhs-news-releases/ibhs-rounds-out-wildfire-prepared-program-with-neighborhood-and-multifamily-standards-updates-home-requirements/)). The first applied neighbourhood is **KB Home's Dixon Trail** ([IBHS](https://ibhs.org/ibhs-news-releases/kb-home-introduces-wildfire-resilient-neighborhood/)). Mercury Insurance formalised its IBHS link in **May 2025** ([Mercury newsroom](https://newsroom.mercuryinsurance.com/mercury-insurance-teams-up-with-ibhs-to-promote-wildfire-preparedness)); Mercury has proposed discounts of up to **39%** on the wildfire peril portion for Wildfire Prepared Home Plus in a pending CDI filing ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/west/2025/05/29/824983.htm)).

**The P2P/mutual graveyard.** This is the most decision-relevant part of the section.

- **Guevara** (UK motor, founded 2013 by Rich Philip, Mike Greer, Kim Miller, Paul Anderson) let customers pool premiums in a "Protection Pool". It **closed in September 2017 after failing to establish a fully capitalised underwriting vehicle** — i.e. it died of the capital and licensing requirement, not of demand ([Insurance Post](https://www.postonline.co.uk/insurer/3313886/start-up-guevara-closes-for-business-after-failing-to-establish-capitalised-underwriting-vehicle); [Insurance Insider](https://www.insuranceinsider.com/article/2876k7h0obqgqxlvzzwu8/p2p-insurer-guevara-shuts-up-shop)).
- **Friendsurance** (Berlin, 2010, Tim Kunde / Sebastian Herfurth / Janis Meyer-Plath) pioneered claims-free cashback pools, and **since 2017 pivoted its growth into digital bancassurance** for banks such as Deutsche Bank ([Wikipedia P2P insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-to-peer_insurance); [The Digital Insurer](https://www.the-digital-insurer.com/dia/insurtech-analysis-friendsurance/)).
- **Teambrella** built a Bitcoin-escrow P2P model where no member's exposure exceeded their wallet ([The Digital Insurer](https://www.the-digital-insurer.com/blog/insurtech-teambrella-and-the-third-wave-of-peer-to-peer-insurance/)). I **could not verify** its current operating status.
- **so-sure** (London MGA, mobile phone) survives as a *referral-bonus* mechanism: up to 8 connected friends, £10 credit each, returned if nobody claims ([Oxbow Partners](https://oxbowpartners.com/blog/peer-to-peer-insurance/)).
- **Laka** (UK, bicycles/micromobility) is the healthiest "collective" model: monthly contributions from a rider community instead of fixed premiums. It raised a **$10.4m Series B** ([Beinsure](https://beinsure.com/news/insurtech-laka-raised-series-b/)), took **£6.5m venture debt in 2025** ([Startup Magazine](https://www.startupmag.co.uk/funding/laka-2025-series-b-funding/)), and **acquired Luko's e-scooter portfolio in 2025** ([Laka Wrapped 2025](https://laka.co/gb/commercial-insurance/resources/laka-wrapped-2025/)). Note the pattern: it works on **small, low-severity, high-frequency, non-correlated** risk.
- **Lemonade** is named by the NAIC as the first US P2P carrier (2015) but is a licensed carrier with a "Giveback", not a member-governed pool ([NAIC](https://content.naic.org/insurance-topics/peer-to-peer-insurance), page updated 24 June 2026).

**The best working analogue in the world is not insurance at all: Broodfonds.** Dutch "bread funds" let self-employed people fund each other's sick pay by *gift*, explicitly **not insurance**, based on trust. Groups have a recommended minimum of **25** and maximum of **50** members. Members save **€33.75–€112.50/month**, plus a **€250** one-off service fee and **€10/month**. As of **mid-2026 there were 677 broodfondsen with roughly 32,000 participants** ([Wikipedia: Broodfonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broodfonds); [EC social protection paper](https://ec.europa.eu/social/BlobServlet?docId=19009&langId=en)).

**And the best cautionary analogue is Health Care Sharing Ministries.** HCSMs are the US precedent for "a pool that is not insurance" at consumer scale — and the precedent for what regulators do about it. **Aliera/Trinity**: regulators in Colorado, Connecticut, New Hampshire, Texas and Washington took action; allegations included committing only **8%–35%** of fees collected to member claims, using insurance terms like "deductible" and "premium", and diverting member funds to officers and affiliates. Aliera claimed ~**100,000** customers nationally ([Georgetown CHIR](https://chir.georgetown.edu/aliera-healthcare-prompts-increased-state-activity-health-care-sharing-ministries/); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2019/11/25/780612410/regulators-allege-christian-based-health-care-provider-broke-state-federal-rules); [Commonwealth Fund](https://www.commonwealthfund.org/blog/2020/states-take-action-health-care-sharing-ministries-more-could-be-done-protect-consumers)).

## 1.2 Traction, with evidence

- Isleton: **$200,000** grant, launched Oct 2024 (state-funded, not member-funded) — independently reported.
- NYC: **$1.1m** cover, **$15,000** max per household, **never triggered** in the study window — the nonprofit's own partner (EDF) says so.
- Franklin LA: payouts "likely around **$2,000**".
- Broodfonds: **677** funds / **~32,000** participants, mid-2026.
- Laka: **$10.4m** Series B; **£6.5m** venture debt 2025; Luko e-scooter book acquired.
- Aliera: **~100,000** customers claimed; 8–35% of fees to claims per regulators.
- Guevara: **dead, Sept 2017**, for want of underwriting capital.

I **could not find** any live, named product in the US, UK or EU in which neighbouring households pool money to fund *both* joint physical prevention work *and* payment of defined property losses. If it exists I did not find it.

## 1.3 What insiders are saying

- **Carolyn Kousky** (founder, Insurance for Good, 2024; co-author of the CBCI framework) frames the funding question directly: if we invest in loss reduction and lower risk, we should see lower premiums, and that saving "could be used as a cash flow to pay for the investments" ([Resources Radio interview](https://www.resources.org/resources-radio/reimagining-homeowners-insurance-with-carolyn-kousky/); [ImpactAlpha Q&A](https://impactalpha.com/innovations-in-risk-transfer-for-climate-vulnerable-communities-a-qa-with-insurance-for-goods-carolyn-kousky/)). This is an individual practitioner's position, not a carrier's.
- **Dr Guillermo Franco**, Global Head of Cat Risk Research, Guy Carpenter, on launching the reciprocal-exchange research: "Insurance plays a critical role in recovery from natural disasters, but many households and small businesses do not have sufficient coverage…" ([Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/guy-carpenter-launches-new-parametric-solutions-research-project-for-california-florida/)). Institutional position of a broker, and notably still at *research* stage on regulation.
- **Bridget Carle**, SVP Public Sector Practice, Guy Carpenter, is the named practitioner associated with the CBCI insight work ([Guy Carpenter](https://www.guycarp.com/insights/community-based-catastrophe-insurance.html)).
- **Commissioner Ricardo Lara** (California DOI), official regulator position on the Isleton pilot: "Insurance is a key driver of financial resilience, and we need to think creatively about how new insurance pilots like this Delta community project can be tested and scaled" ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/catastrophe/california-launches-firstever-communitybased-flood-program-510367.aspx)).
- **Carolyn Kousky and Xuesong You** (Insurance for Good) on the mitigation-credit mechanism that Micromutual implicitly relies on: filed discounts run "from a fraction of a percent to over 40%" for the same measure, and "insurer confidence in the evidence base around the effectiveness of different measures may vary considerably" ([Epicenter Insights summary](https://www.epicenterinsights.com/the-weekly-the-messy-math-of-wildfire-mitigation-discounts/); [Insurance for Good](https://www.insuranceforgood.org/blog/do-ca-insurers-reward-wildfire-resilience)). Under Safer from Wildfires the regulation **did not mandate discount levels, only that insurers offer something** — a critical caveat.
- The NAIC's own P2P page raises exactly one risk: **basis risk**, when claims exceed collected premiums and reinsurance must absorb the shortfall ([NAIC](https://content.naic.org/insurance-topics/peer-to-peer-insurance)). That is the pamphlet's page-6 "if the pool falls short" box, named by the regulators' own body.

## 1.4 Does it line up with our story?

**Supported by the evidence:**
- The physical premise is real and now formally recognised. IBHS built a neighbourhood standard in June 2026 precisely because structure-to-structure spread and cross-boundary fuel cannot be fixed one lot at a time. The "branches don't stop at your fence" page is correct.
- "Alongside your insurance, not replacing it" is the right architecture and matches every live pilot. Isleton is explicitly a supplement to existing cover.
- Members wanting to see the plan, vote a budget and follow the money (page 5) mirrors how Broodfonds actually works, and Broodfonds is the most durable member-governed money pool in this space.
- The pamphlet's honesty on page 6 — "your insurer would not automatically cover a pool shortfall" — is the same risk the NAIC flags. Good instinct.

**Contradicted or complicated:**
- **The prevention-discount route is already occupied and delivers weakly.** A homeowner does not need a pool to get community credit; Firewise USA participation already qualifies under Safer from Wildfires. And the credit for it can be "a fraction of a percent". If the pamphlet's implicit value proposition is "pool money, do the work, get rewarded", the reward channel exists and is currently unreliable.
- **Every live community programme is funded by government or philanthropy, not by neighbours.** Isleton is a $200k state grant. NYC was a nonprofit-bought policy. Franklin is a consortium of public bodies. The concept's central financial act — households voluntarily contributing to a pool — is the one part nobody has demonstrated at property-loss scale.
- **"Defined smaller losses" is where the regulator bites.** Paying $4,500 toward a neighbour's fence repair from pooled contributions, under written rules with professional assessment and appeal, is functionally indemnity insurance. Guevara died at exactly this line.
- **The $5,000 fence example is an awkward choice.** A storm-damaged fence is usually *within* a US homeowners policy (Coverage B, other structures) subject to deductible. The pamphlet has to posit a gap that may not commonly exist, which will read as contrived to an insurance audience. **Inference**, based on standard HO-3 structure; I did not source a coverage-form analysis.
- **Small pools cannot absorb correlated loss.** Broodfonds caps groups at 50 and covers *sickness* — idiosyncratic, individually verifiable, low severity. A wildfire or windstorm hits every member of a neighbourhood pool simultaneously. Laka works for bikes for the same reason Broodfonds works for illness. A geographically-defined pool for weather losses inverts the property that makes both viable. This is the concept's deepest unsolved problem.

**Assumptions nobody in the market has solved:**
1. Getting households to fund a prevention pool voluntarily when the insurance saving is uncertain and may be sub-1%.
2. Legally holding and paying member money for property losses without a carrier licence or capital. Nobody has done it for property in the US.
3. Governance on a street. Who overrules the neighbour who won't allow the arborist on their land? Consent is assumed on page 4 ("with each owner's permission") and consent is the thing that fails.
4. Shortfall. Named by the NAIC, acknowledged by the pamphlet, solved by nobody.

## 1.5 What this means for a prototype

**Must demonstrate:**
1. **The legal wrapper, on screen.** Show *who holds the money and under what authority* — a special district, a nonprofit facilitator buying a group policy from a licensed carrier, or a discretionary fund that never promises to pay. An insurance audience will ask this in the first 90 seconds and nothing else matters until it is answered.
2. **A prevention-first, loss-payment-second framing.** Make joint mitigation the core (which is legal, evidenced and now IBHS-standardised) and make loss payment a clearly-bounded, capped, discretionary tail. Show the cap and the shortfall rule in the interface, not the footnotes.
3. **Correlation honestly.** One screen showing what the pool does when *ten* members claim at once. If the prototype can show a pool with a fixed aggregate limit and a pro-rata rule, it will read as credible rather than naive.

**Avoid claiming:** that the pool "covers" anything (use "may pay, up to"); that it substitutes for insurance; that it reduces premiums by a specific amount; and avoid the word *mutual* if the entity is not one.

## 1.6 What would change our mind

- A named US programme where **residents' own contributions** — not grant or philanthropic money — fund property-loss payments, with a state insurance department having blessed the structure.
- Evidence that a carrier will give a **material, filed, verifiable** credit (>10% of total premium, not of the wildfire component) for participation in a *pool*, as distinct from Firewise membership.
- A Broodfonds-style fund that has survived one correlated event with members still enrolled afterwards.

# 2. Emberwatch

> *Opt-in insurer wildfire service: conditions monitoring, optional drone check, optional automatic sprinklers, a human check-in call, route guidance.*

## 2.1 Who is already doing this

**This is the most crowded of the six. Four of the five Emberwatch pages describe things that already exist commercially.**

**Insurer-funded wildfire response — live for years.** Chubb and American Family partner with **Wildfire Defense Systems** (Montana) to protect policyholders when fire threatens; Chubb and **USAA** both contract with WDS. Chubb's programme covers homes with replacement value of **$1m or more** and subcontracts a pool of about **50 fire engines** across the West dedicated to Chubb policyholders, across **19 states** ([Chubb](https://www.chubb.com/us-en/individuals-families/products/natural-disasters/wildfire-defense.html); [Insurance.com](https://www.insurance.com/home-insurance/wildfire-insurance/wildfire-defense-insurance-services/); [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/chubb/chubb-sends-private-firefighters-to-help-protect-homeowners-83985.aspx)). Neither Chubb nor AmFam charges homeowners extra; **American Family auto-enrols** eligible homeowners while **Chubb requires opt-in** ([Insurance.com](https://www.insurance.com/home-insurance/wildfire-insurance/wildfire-defense-insurance-services/)) — note that the opt-in/auto-enrol design choice the pamphlet treats as novel has already been made both ways in the market.

**The sprinkler page is a shipped product with a named insurer partnership.** **Frontline Wildfire Defense** sells automated exterior wildfire defence — zoned sprinklers, Class-A biodegradable foam, battery backup, satellite connectivity, remote monitoring dashboard. It closed a **$48m Series A led by Norwest, announced 28 October 2025** ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/frontline-wildfire-defense-closes-48m-series-a-to-protect-homes-businesses-and-communities-amid-escalating-wildfire-risk-302596802.html)). On **26 May 2026**, **Stand** and Frontline announced a partnership in which Stand offers premium discounts for installing Frontline's system, with Stand's physics-based model showing **up to a 60% decrease in modelled risk**, available statewide in California subject to capacity ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/stand-and-frontline-wildfire-defense-launch-innovative-partnership-to-lower-insurance-premiums-with-proactive-wildfire-defense-technology-302781470.html)).
  - **Company claim, flagged:** "Frontline Defense Systems protected 96% of its customers' homes" during the 2025 LA wildfires — this is Frontline's own figure, in its own release. Do not repeat it as independent.

**Stand** is the closest thing to an insurer built on the Emberwatch thesis. It builds physics-based digital twins of properties to simulate fire, flood and storm behaviour; policies are issued by **Concert Specialty Insurance Company** and reinsured by **Arch** and **RenaissanceRe** ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/stand-and-frontline-wildfire-defense-launch-innovative-partnership-to-lower-insurance-premiums-with-proactive-wildfire-defense-technology-302781470.html)). It raised a **$35m Series B led by Eclipse**, with Lowercarbon Capital and Inspired Capital also backing, and has about **$1bn of insured property value** in wildfire-prone California, expanding to Florida ([Beinsure summary of the round](https://beinsure.com/news/stand-secures-35m-series-b/); [Insurance Journal, 17 Oct 2025](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2025/10/17/844092.htm); [Bloomberg, 16 Oct 2025](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-16/san-francisco-based-insurance-startup-plans-expansion-in-risky-markets)).

**The conditions-monitoring page is the most contested space of all.**
- **Delos Insurance Solutions** (founded 2017 by aerospace engineers) is a wildfire MGA using proprietary modelling and satellite imagery. In **October 2025** it announced wildfire-model confidence enabling insurance for **265,000 additional homes** across five Southern California counties, taking its footprint to about **2.5 million homes** in LA, Orange, Riverside, San Bernardino and San Diego; it manages **$40bn** of total insured value; funding led by **HSBC Asset Management** with IA Capital Group, Blue Bear Capital and Generation Space ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/delos-wildfire-science-opens-up-insurance-for-265-000-more-southern-california-homes-302591607.html); earlier rounds: **$5.3m** 2021 [Carrier Management](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2021/12/01/229822.htm), **$9m** [Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/property-mga-delos-raises-9m-to-expand-wildfire-insurance-for-homeowners/)).
- **Faura** (founded 2023) sells property *survivability* analytics from aerial imagery, hazard models and policyholder-supplied property data; **$3.5m seed** co-led by Harlem Capital and Building Ventures with MetaProp, Triple Five and Dorm Room Fund, **$4.43m** total raised; expanded to all five NatCat perils; **October 2025** partnership with **Insurity** embedding its resilience data into carrier underwriting, pricing and claims workflows; CB Insights Top 50 Insurtech 2025 ([Faura press](https://www.faura.us/press/faura-raises-35m-seed-round-to-transform-climate-risk-and-property-insurance); [Insurance Innovation Reporter](https://iireporter.com/insurity-faura-partner-to-integrate-resilience-data-into-underwriting/); [Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2025/02/insurtech-la-fires-faura/)). Faura claims 90–95% predictive accuracy in wildfire case studies including Palisades and Eaton — **company claim**.

**The alerting and route-guidance pages are owned by a nonprofit and by public agencies, at a scale no insurer will match.** **Watch Duty** was downloaded **2 million times** in the week of the January 2025 LA fires, had **14 million unique users** that week, added **600,000 users in a single day**, was the **top free app on iOS and Google Play in the US for roughly five days**, briefly **surpassing ChatGPT**, and had **7.2 million yearly active users in 2024** — run by a nonprofit with **15 full-time staff and 200 volunteers** ([TechCrunch 12 Jan 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/12/watch-duty-was-downloaded-2-million-times-during-this-weeks-la-fires/); [TechCrunch 9 Jan 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/09/watch-duty-surpasses-chatgpt-as-top-free-app-on-app-store-as-california-fires-spread); [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2025/01/09/watch-duty-wildfires-app-la-california-fires/)).

**The drone page has a specific, brutal counter-example from the exact fire this project replays.** On **9 January 2025** a hobbyist launched a drone from a Santa Monica parking structure to survey Palisades fire damage, inside an **FAA Temporary Flight Restriction** running through 24 January. It struck a Canadair CL-415 Super Scooper, punching a hole in the wing, **grounding all firefighting aircraft on the Palisades fire temporarily** and taking one of only two Super Scoopers out of service for about five days. **Peter Tripp Akemann pleaded guilty** to unsafe operation of an unmanned aircraft; interfering with firefighting on public lands carries up to **12 months** imprisonment, and the FAA can levy civil penalties up to **$75,000** for TFR interference ([NBC Los Angeles](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/california-wildfires/drone-collision-super-scooper-palisades-fire/3620389/); [The War Zone](https://www.twz.com/air/one-of-just-two-cl-415-super-scooper-planes-taken-out-of-palisades-fire-fight-by-drone); [ABC7](https://abc7.com/post/los-angeles-fires-super-scooper-firefighting-aircraft-hit-drone-flying-palisades-fire-faa-investigating/15785774/)).

## 2.3 What insiders are saying

- **Harry Statter**, CEO, Frontline: "The insurance crisis in California is not going to be solved by smarter pricing alone. Our system monitors conditions in real time and responds when it matters." ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/stand-and-frontline-wildfire-defense-launch-innovative-partnership-to-lower-insurance-premiums-with-proactive-wildfire-defense-technology-302781470.html))
- **Dan Preston**, CEO, Stand: "We updated their property model to show how much safer their home is." (same release) — the interesting thing here is what it concedes: the value is in *repricing the model*, not in the service experience.
- **Cal Fire** has said private firefighters can pose a safety risk, that private crews were entering evacuation areas without prior authorisation and in a couple of instances had to be rescued, putting emergency personnel at risk. A **2018 California law** requires private firefighters entering an evacuation zone to check in with the local incident commander and follow instructions, including leaving when told ([CalMatters, Jan 2025](https://calmatters.org/environment/wildfires/2025/01/private-firefighters-insurers/)).
- The **equity backlash is documented and attached to this exact fire**: Rick Caruso's private crews defending Palisades Village drew criticism as images circulated of intact chain stores beside burnt-out homes and small businesses ([CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/environment/wildfires/2025/01/private-firefighters-insurers/); [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2025/01/12/private-firefighters-la-wildfires-palisades-fire-insurance-companies)).
- **Commissioner Lara**'s October 2025 legislative package is the current regulatory direction of travel, and it is about **funding mitigation and standardising credits**, not about insurer-delivered emergency services: **AB 888** (California Safe Homes Act, grants for fire-safe roofs and Zone Zero work), **AB 1** (Insurance and Wildfire Safety Act, mandating regular updates to Safer from Wildfires so discounts stay meaningful), **SB 429** (public wildfire catastrophe model), **SB 495** (60% contents coverage up to $350,000 without an inventory after total loss), **SB 547**, **AB 226** (FAIR Plan cat bonds and credit lines) ([CDI, 13 Oct 2025](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0400-news/0102-alerts/2025/Commissioner-Lara-Announces-New-Wildfire.cfm)).
- **Kousky / You** again, on the mechanism Emberwatch would be monetised through: pricing is not consistently reflecting risk and firms are not accurately measuring the loss-reduction value of home hardening ([Epicenter Insights](https://www.epicenterinsights.com/the-weekly-the-messy-math-of-wildfire-mitigation-discounts/)).

## 2.4 Does it line up with our story?

**Supported:**
- Insurer-provided pre-loss wildfire service is real, mainstream, free to the policyholder, and delivered by major carriers. The concept is not fanciful.
- Consent-layered permissions (page 2) match how these programmes actually work — Chubb opt-in vs AmFam auto-enrol is a live design debate.
- The "conditions portrait" of weather, wind, fire activity, terrain, vegetation and shared home data (page 3) is exactly what Delos, Faura and Stand sell to underwriters today. The data layer is credible and buildable — which happens to be what the Project WildFire repo is already assembling.
- Automatic sprinklers as an insurer-blessed, premium-affecting intervention is **shipped as of May 2026**. This is the strongest single validation of any page across all six concepts.

**Contradicted or complicated:**
- **The drone page is close to indefensible in the January 2025 Palisades context.** Airspace over an active wildfire is TFR-restricted; a drone in that airspace grounded the air attack on this very fire and produced a federal guilty plea. The pamphlet's hedge — "a safe, permitted flight" — is doing enormous work. An insurance or emergency-management audience will read the drone page as the moment the concept stopped being serious. Mitigation is possible (pre-season assessment flights, post-event claims flights, or agency-integrated operations under a COA) but that is a *different* product page from "a drone could check nearby conditions" during an event.
- **Route guidance is an agency function and a liability magnet.** Evacuation zones and orders come from local authorities; the pamphlet correctly defers to them on every page, which raises the obvious question of what the insurer is adding. **Inference:** voluntarily undertaking to guide a household's evacuation creates a duty where none existed — the classic negligent-undertaking exposure. I looked for a wildfire-specific case on insurer evacuation advice and **found none**; the nearest documented doctrine is that agents and insurers have no duty to advise, but become liable once they do advise and get it wrong ([Merlin Law Group](https://www.merlinlawgroup.com/liability-on-the-insurance-agent/)). Treat the duty-creation point as reasoning, not as a found holding.
- **The conditions-monitoring proposition is already saturated and the incumbent is free.** Watch Duty's scale — 14 million uniques in one week, top of both app stores for five days — means an insurer-branded alerting app is entering a market where the best product is a nonprofit and costs nothing. "Our app tells you about the fire" is not differentiated in California in 2026.
- **The economics are unproven at the discount end.** The mechanism by which Emberwatch pays for itself is a mitigation credit, and filed credits currently range from under 1% to over 40% for the same measure, with the regulator not having mandated levels. Actuarial evidence for specific measures is acknowledged as thin ([Insurance for Good](https://www.insuranceforgood.org/blog/do-ca-insurers-reward-wildfire-resilience); [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/financial-protection-against-catastrophic-risks_32a3acd7-en/full-report/insurance-coverage-for-wildfire-risk_f5877210.html)).
- **The reputational trap is live, specific and attached to Pacific Palisades.** A service that protects some homes and not others, delivered by an insurer, in the Palisades, in January 2025, is the exact fact pattern that produced the Caruso backlash. The pamphlet's emotional register ("Your family. Your pets. A lifetime of memories") will amplify rather than defuse this.

**Unsolved by anyone:**
1. Whether the human check-in call is net-positive. I **found no** evidence of any insurer running proactive pre-evacuation welfare calls at scale, and no published evaluation. It may be the most genuinely novel page in the pamphlet — and also the one where an insurer employee could say something that costs a life.
2. Whether automatic sprinklers work when the water main is depressurised or power is out. Frontline sells battery backup and satellite connectivity, which implies the failure modes are known; the pamphlet's "They need suitable water and power" is honest but the prototype must make it vivid.
3. Verified attribution of survival to intervention. Frontline's 96% is self-reported. Nobody has published an independent study.

## 2.5 What this means for a prototype

**Must demonstrate:**
1. **Deference architecture, mechanically.** Every screen should show the *official* order as the authoritative object and the insurer's content as subordinate and timestamped against it. If the prototype can show a conflict case — insurer signal says one thing, agency order says another, and the UI resolves to the agency — it will earn credibility instantly. That is also the honest answer to the liability question.
2. **Provenance and time-boxing on the risk state.** Show each signal's source and observation time, and show what was *not* known at that moment. An insurance audience is trained to ask "what did you know and when". This lines up exactly with the repo's existing provenance and no-future-leakage principles.
3. **One pre-committed, automatable action with a priced value.** Sprinklers are the defensible choice, because Stand and Frontline have already established that a carrier will reprice for it. Show the action, the authorisation, and the premium consequence.

**Avoid claiming:** live drone flights over an active fire; any survival percentage; that the service replaces or improves on official evacuation instructions; that the insurer will "get you out". Also avoid framing that implies triage between policyholders — the Palisades backlash makes that the single biggest reputational risk in the set.

**Consider cutting the drone page entirely** and replacing it with a pre-season assessment flight, which is legal, boring, and real.

## 2.6 What would change our mind

- A named insurer running documented **during-event UAS operations** inside a wildfire TFR under an FAA authorisation, or an FAA pathway that makes that routine.
- An **independent** (not carrier, not vendor) study attributing home survival to an automated sprinkler or defence intervention.
- Evidence that households will pay, or accept a materially higher premium, for the monitoring-and-call layer specifically — as distinct from taking free Watch Duty alerts.
- A published evaluation of proactive insurer welfare calls during an evacuation showing improved outcomes and no adverse events.

# 3. Employment Insurance

> *A career plan plus coaching while still employed, and limited income support after a qualifying job loss.*

## 3.1 Who is already doing this

**The US product category exists, is tiny, and has already failed once in living memory.**

- **IncomeAssure** — the reference case. Launched **2011**, distributed by **SterlingRisk**, underwritten by **Great American Insurance**, paying a weekly benefit equal to **50% of former income** on top of state unemployment benefit, marketed hardest in states that had cut benefit duration (Florida, Georgia, Michigan, Missouri, South Carolina) ([SterlingRisk](https://www.sterlingrisk.com/incomeassure-offers-private-solutions-to-reduced-unemployment-benefit-periods/)). It was the **largest private unemployment insurance policy in the US** and had **about 1,000 active policyholders and just over $1m in claims paid as of 2016**, and **stopped accepting new applications in late 2018** ([The Billfold, Nicole Dieker](https://medium.com/the-billfold/a-private-unemployment-insurance-update-12023e2e3952) — I could not fetch the article body, which returned 403; the figures come from the search index of that piece and are consistent across secondary coverage, so treat the 1,000/\$1m figures as **medium confidence, single-source**).
- **Walnut** is the live US distribution layer today: an embedded insurance platform offering supplemental unemployment insurance covering **up to 50% of pre-tax weekly income**, plus mortgage-specific job-loss cover, via "over a dozen insurance carriers". Pricing indications: **~1% of annual salary** (example given: **$50/month** on a $60,000 salary); mortgage-specific **2%–5% of the monthly housing payment**; typically a **60-day waiting period**; excludes resignation and termination for cause ([Walnut](https://www.gowalnut.com/insight/economic-uncertainty-and-why-now-is-the-time-for-job-loss-insurance)). Walnut names no underwriter and publishes no traction figures. I **could not verify** how much of this is bound business versus marketing.
- General market description: the closest options are supplemental job-loss policies from niche providers, but **many have paused new enrolments**, with limited availability and high cost ([Insurance.com](https://www.insurance.com/home-and-renters-insurance/coverage/job-loss-insurance-mortgage.html)).

**The AI-displacement entrant is the most direct competitor to the concept's framing.** **Singularity** (Y Combinator backed) launched **SingularityShield Income Cover** in **May 2025**: a parametric income-protection policy with a **dual trigger** — its proprietary **AI-Displacement Risk Index (AIDR)** exceeding an agreed threshold, *and* evidence of involuntary separation. Pays **up to 50% of net pay on each scheduled payday** over **3, 6 or 12 months**, with no adjuster and instant confirmation. AIDR is built from corporate filings, earnings releases, call transcripts, government data and market signals, with weightings published. Capacity described as **Lloyd's capacity and US admitted carriers** (partnerships *planned*). Initially for knowledge workers in the **UK and US** ([Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/artificial-intelligence-taken-your-job-theres-a-parametric-insurance-policy-for-that/)). I **could not verify** whether it is now writing business or how much.

**The overseas evidence is where this concept actually works, and it is bundled with collective membership.**
- **Sweden.** Union complementary income insurance (*inkomstförsäkring*) tops up the state *a-kassa* so that members can receive **up to 80% of full salary**, covering earnings above about **SEK 34,000/month**; Unionen members can insure income up to **SEK 60,000/month** for up to **150 days**; a-kassa membership at about **SEK 140/month** is a *precondition* for the top-up to be valid ([Unionen](https://www.unionen.se/nu/unionens-income-insurance); [Akademikernas a-kassa](https://www.akademikernasakassa.se/en/membership/income-insurance-through-your-union); [Sveriges Ingenjörer](https://www.sverigesingenjorer.se/medlemskap/incomeinsurance/); academic overview: [Social Policy & Administration](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/spol.12943)).
- **Netherlands.** **Broodfonds** — see §1.1. Covers sickness, not unemployment, but is the proof that member-funded income replacement can run at scale (**677 funds, ~32,000 participants**, mid-2026) when it is *not* insurance and is bound by group size and trust.
- **Coaching and outplacement** as a standalone service is a mature B2B industry sold to employers, not to households. I **did not** research provider-level traction here; flagging it as a known adjacent market rather than asserting figures.

**Nobody found combining both halves.** I searched specifically for a startup or carrier offering *career coaching before job loss plus income support after it* as one consumer product, across 2025–2026, and **could not find one**. The nearest 2026 activity is adjacent and non-insurance: in **March 2026** the nonprofit **What We Will** partnered with the **Fund for Guaranteed Income** on what organisers describe as the first basic income programme designed specifically for workers ([Metaintro roundup](https://www.metaintro.com/blog/ai-displaced-workers-support-programs-2026) — secondary source, low confidence on the characterisation).

**And the cautionary tale is the largest consumer redress event in British history.** UK **Payment Protection Insurance** — originally sold in the industry as **accident, sickness and unemployment insurance** — produced **£38.3bn** paid out to mis-sold customers between 2011 and 2020, with the claims window closing **29 August 2019** ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/the-ppi-scandal-is-far-from-over-heres-why-144688); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/isabeltogoh/2019/08/29/ppi-in-numbers-a-look-at-the-scale-of-britains-59-billion-consumer-scandal/)). Causes cited: added without being told, not presented as optional, lender profitability, and pressurised staff incentives.

## 3.2 Traction, with evidence

- IncomeAssure: **~1,000 policyholders**, **>$1m claims paid** by 2016, **closed to new business late 2018** — single-source, medium confidence.
- Walnut: "over a dozen carriers"; **~1% of salary** pricing; **60-day** wait. No policy counts published.
- Singularity: launched **May 2025**; **50% of net pay**; 3/6/12-month terms; Lloyd's + US admitted capacity described as planned partnerships. No bound-business figures found.
- Sweden: **up to 80%** replacement combined; **SEK 60,000/month** insurable at Unionen; **150 days**. I **could not find** an aggregate insured-member count across all Swedish unions.
- Broodfonds: **677 funds**, **~32,000 participants** (mid-2026); **€33.75–€112.50/month** savings, **€250** joining fee, **€10/month**; groups of **25–50**.
- PPI: **£38.3bn** redress 2011–2020.

## 3.3 What insiders are saying

The most consequential "insider" view here is the economics literature, and it is unusually unanimous.

- **Adverse selection is measured, not theoretical.** Swedish evidence: unemployment risk is **more than twice as high** for workers who buy supplemental coverage, with **25–30% of that correlation driven by risk-based selection** ([CEPR/VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/unemployment-insurance-and-adverse-selection-evidence-sweden); [AER](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.20180820)).
- **Workers have private advance information about their own job loss** — the canonical problem for voluntary unemployment cover ([Hendren, AER](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/aer.20151655%20insurance.pdf); [JPubE](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0047272723001287)).
- **Correlated risk is the fatal one.** Unemployment risks are positively correlated across workers, so private insurers may not survive adverse macro shocks producing sharp, long unemployment spikes ([Holmlund, theoretical aspects of UI](https://bm.dk/media/6656/theoretical_aspects_unemployment_-insurance-pdf.pdf)). This, not moral hazard, is why UI is compulsory and public almost everywhere.
- **On the AI-displacement variant**, Artemis reports Singularity's own framing that the dual trigger "ensures payouts are directly linked to AI's impact on job roles", against WEF's estimate that **92 million jobs** may be displaced by 2030 and a claim that **52%** of US workers fear AI's employment impact ([Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/artificial-intelligence-taken-your-job-theres-a-parametric-insurance-policy-for-that/)). These are the company's and WEF's numbers, not an underwriter's.
- **Context for an insurance audience specifically:** insurance-sector employment itself is contracting, with reporting of a **21% year-over-year** sector employment drop and entry-level adjuster positions down **50% YoY** ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/breaking-news/insurance-keeps-losing-jobs--ai-is-only-part-of-the-story-589149.aspx)). Present this concept to insurance people and they will hear it as being about themselves. **Inference:** that is an asset for engagement and a risk for objectivity.

## 3.4 Does it line up with our story?

**Supported:**
- The *sequence* — plan and coach while still employed, pay only after a qualifying loss reviewed for eligibility — is the right structural answer to the private-information problem. Selling readiness before the event and money after it is coherent.
- Page 7's honesty is the strongest writing in any of the six pamphlets: "A bridge. Not a promise of a job", payments follow an eligibility review "not a warning that work may change", limits, and "review government benefits and employer support alongside this protection". That is PPI-proofing, and it is deliberate.
- Page 5's consent boundaries — no employer contact without permission, no applications sent on her behalf — are well judged. Employer contact is the thing that would make this product radioactive.
- The 50%-of-income benefit shape matches what the market actually offers (IncomeAssure, Walnut, Singularity all land on ~50%).

**Contradicted or complicated:**
- **Customers have repeatedly declined to buy this.** The largest private unemployment insurance product in the US reached roughly a thousand people and closed to new business. That is not a distribution failure story you can wave away; it is the base rate.
- **The one geography where it works is the one the pamphlet's structure does not describe.** Sweden's income insurance is effectively compulsory-by-bundling: it rides on union membership and requires a-kassa membership to be valid. Take away the collective and the group underwriting and you are back to IncomeAssure. **Inference:** the viable version of this concept is probably B2B2C — through an employer, a union, a professional association or a lender — not direct-to-Maya.
- **Maya's own premise contains the adverse selection.** Page 2 has her acting *because* "a reorganization makes her want to prepare for a possible change". That is precisely the private advance information that the Hendren literature identifies as unpriceable. The pamphlet's most sympathetic narrative beat is also its underwriting problem, and a sophisticated audience will spot it immediately.
- **The name is a regulatory hazard in the US.** "Employment Insurance" is the actual name of Canada's *state* scheme, and "unemployment insurance" in the US denotes the mandatory federal-state programme. **Inference:** a private product using either phrase invites confusion and regulator attention. Compare the Aliera finding, where using insurance vocabulary was itself part of the enforcement case.
- **AI-displacement framing is now occupied and carries a triggerability problem.** A parametric index of AI displacement (AIDR) is clever but basis risk cuts both ways: Maya loses her nursing job in a reorganisation that has nothing to do with AI, the index does not fire, and she gets nothing. The pamphlet avoids this by using an eligibility review instead of an index, which is more honest and more expensive to run.

**Unsolved by anyone:**
1. Pricing voluntary job-loss cover for individuals with private information, without either a group mandate or a very long waiting period.
2. Surviving a recession. Nobody has a private consumer unemployment book that has been through a downturn with capital intact — **not found**, and I would treat any claim otherwise with suspicion.
3. Proving the coaching half creates value. I **found no** study of whether pre-loss career coaching reduces claim duration or severity. That would be the underwriting case for the whole product and it appears not to exist publicly.

## 3.5 What this means for a prototype

**Must demonstrate:**
1. **The eligibility gate as a first-class object.** Show what counts as a qualifying loss, the waiting period, the exclusions, and the decision record. The prototype's credibility rests entirely on the boundary between "work may change" and "a qualifying event occurred".
2. **The relationship between coaching and the benefit.** If coaching participation is a condition of payment, show it as a condition. If it is not, show why the insurer funds it anyway — reduced duration, faster reemployment. Pick one and make it explicit; an underwriter will not accept "it's nice to have".
3. **Distribution context.** Put the prototype inside an employer, union or lender relationship rather than a standalone consumer purchase. This is the single change most likely to move an insurance audience from "we tried that, it was IncomeAssure" to "this is a group product".

**Avoid claiming:** the words "unemployment insurance" or "employment insurance" as the product name; any replacement rate above about 50%; any implication of job placement; and any suggestion that a warning of restructuring triggers anything. Also avoid a PPI-shaped sale — bundled, opt-out, incentivised — and be seen to avoid it.

## 3.6 What would change our mind

- A private consumer job-loss book of meaningful size (say >100,000 lives) that has traded through a recession, in any market.
- Published evidence that pre-loss coaching shortens unemployment duration enough to fund itself.
- A US group structure — employer-sponsored SUB-style arrangement, association or union scheme — that a state regulator has explicitly approved for voluntary supplemental unemployment benefit at consumer scale.
- Singularity publishing bound premium and a paid claim under the AIDR trigger.

# 4. Agent Error Protection

> *Protection and remedy when an AI agent you delegated to makes a costly mistake.*

## 4.1 Who is already doing this

**The market is real, moving very fast, and almost entirely commercial rather than consumer.**

| Player | What it does | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Armilla AI** | First Lloyd's coverholder dedicated exclusively to AI liability (2024, via Lloyd's Lab). Standalone AI liability policy launched **April 2025**, covering AI-specific risks including hallucinations, model drift and deviation from expected behaviour. Limits **up to $25m** per organisation, underwritten by certain underwriters at Lloyd's. Raised **$25m** to expand, reported **23 January 2026** | Live; clients from AI scale-ups to Fortune 1000 ([Fintech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/01/23/armilla-ai-raises-25m-to-expand-ai-liability-coverage/); [The Insurer](https://www.theinsurer.com/program-manager/news/standalone-ai-liability-market-takes-shape-with-underwriting-discipline-key-to-2026-04-24/)) |
| **Testudo** | Lloyd's Lab (Cohort 14) MGA founded by ex-Goldman Sachs technologists; began underwriting **January 2026**, US mid-market AI liabilities; capacity expanded with **Atrium and QBE on 26 February 2026** | Live ([The Insurer](https://www.theinsurer.com/program-manager/news/standalone-ai-liability-market-takes-shape-with-underwriting-discipline-key-to-2026-04-24/)) |
| **HSB (Munich Re)** | Introduced AI liability insurance for **small businesses** in **March 2026**, covering bodily injury, property damage and advertising injury arising from AI use | Live (same source) |
| **AIUC** | Pairs the **AIUC-1** certification standard with third-party audits and insurance. The **ElevenLabs AIUC-1 policy (February 2026)** is described as the first binding AI agent insurance policy anchored in a published certification standard, underwritten through Lloyd's | Reported, but I could only find this on secondary/SEO sources — **treat as unverified** pending a trade-press or company confirmation |
| **Apollo Syndicate 1971 "ibott"** | Autonomous/Agentic Liability product announced **12 March 2026** | Announced (secondary source) |

**Consumer side: I found exactly one significant player, and it is not an insurer.**

**American Express** introduced the **Agentic Commerce Experiences (ACE) Developer Kit** and **Amex Agent Purchase Protection** in **April 2026** — described by Amex as an industry-first commitment to back Card Member purchases made by **registered** AI agents across its network. The stated mechanism: if a Card Member authorises a registered AI agent to make a purchase and the agent sends American Express the customer's **authenticated purchase intent**, Amex will protect eligible customers from charges related to **AI agent error**. Quoted: **Luke Gebb**, EVP and head of global innovation — "As commerce becomes more agent-powered, trust becomes the defining factor" — and chairman/CEO **Stephen J. Squeri** ([Amex newsroom](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/newsroom/articles/innovation/american-express-debuts-agentic-commerce-experiences--ace--devel.html); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/american-express-to-back-purchases-made-by-customers-ai-agents/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/14/american-express-ai-payments-developers-purchase-protection/); [Nilson Report](https://nilsonreport.com/articles/amex-to-protect-cardholders-against-ai-agent-errors/)). **No dollar limits, exclusions or launch date were published** — the protection is forward-looking ("will protect"). That is an important gap and also an opportunity.

**The rails are being built around, not for, this risk.**
- **Visa Intelligent Commerce Connect** launched **April 2026**, giving agent platforms one integration point for onboarding, verification and token issuance across major agent protocols; in pilot with broader rollout planned. **Mastercard Agent Pay Acceptance Framework** is already integrated into some processor platforms. **EMVCo** has a dedicated task force on agentic payments ([Worldpay, Reshmi Suresh, Head of Agentic Commerce](https://www.worldpay.com/en/insights/articles/agentic-commerce-liability-is-still-being-written)).
- Worldpay's own assessment: liability rules **have not changed** for agents, so existing frameworks apply, and where agents authenticate properly "liability generally follows existing authenticated tokenized-transaction rules". But: "Where it gets murkier is everything short of outright fraud" — agent misinterpretation, wrong purchases and out-of-scope actions lack clear allocation between issuers, merchants and agent platforms, and **US Regulation E and EU PSD2/SCA contain no agentic-commerce provisions** (same source).

**The existing consumer remedies the pamphlet does not mention.**
- **US DOT 24-hour rule**: for reservations made **seven days or more** before departure, carriers must either hold the fare for 24 hours without payment, or allow cancellation within 24 hours with a full refund and no penalty ([DOT guidance](https://www.transportation.gov/sites/dot.gov/files/docs/Notice_24hour_hold_final20130530.pdf); [DOT refunds page](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds)). **Crucial caveat that cuts the other way:** the requirement **does not apply to tickets booked through online travel agencies, travel agents or other third-party agents** (same sources) — and an AI booking agent may well sit on the wrong side of that line.
- Card chargeback rights under Reg Z/Reg E in the US, and Section 75 / chargeback in the UK. I did **not** source a legal analysis of whether an agent-initiated purchase the cardholder authorised in general terms is "unauthorised" for Reg E purposes. That is a genuine open question and a good research commission.

**Insurers are actively removing AI exposure from the policies consumers and businesses already hold.** ISO/Verisk published **three optional endorsements effective January 2026**: **CG 40 47** (excludes CGL Coverage A and B for bodily injury, property damage and personal/advertising injury arising out of generative AI), **CG 40 48** (Coverage B only), and **CG 35 08** (products/completed operations). Generative AI is defined in the forms as "a machine-based learning system or model that is trained on data with the ability to create content or responses, including but not limited to text, images, audio, video or code" ([Independent Agent / Virtual University](https://www.independentagent.com/vu_resource/verisk-to-roll-out-new-general-liability-exclusions-for-generative-ai-exposures/); analysis: [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/end-silent-ai-emerging-ai-exclusions-coverage-fragmentation-and-practical-implications)). Verisk was reported in **July 2026** to be weighing further exclusions specifically for **agentic** AI risks ([The Insurer](https://www.theinsurer.com/ti/news/verisk-weighs-new-exclusions-for-agentic-ai-risks-2026-07-10/)); insurer interest in AI exclusions is described as growing as the risk becomes omnipresent ([Claims Journal, 20 July 2026](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/07/20/338950.htm)).

**I could not find any personal-lines (homeowners, renters, standalone consumer) product covering AI agent errors, anywhere.** Searches for personal-lines AI endorsements returned only commercial analysis. This is the concept's clearest white space and its clearest warning sign simultaneously.

## 4.2 Traction, with evidence

- Armilla: **$25m** raise (23 Jan 2026); **$25m** policy limits; standalone product live since **April 2025**; first AI-dedicated Lloyd's coverholder (2024).
- Testudo: underwriting from **Jan 2026**; capacity expanded with **Atrium and QBE, 26 Feb 2026**.
- HSB/Munich Re: small-business AI liability, **March 2026**.
- Amex: **April 2026** announcement, protection promised, **no published limits or date**.
- Visa: **Intelligent Commerce Connect, April 2026**, in pilot.
- ISO/Verisk: **CG 40 47 / CG 40 48 / CG 35 08**, effective **January 2026**, optional; **agentic-specific** exclusions under consideration as of July 2026.
- Premium volumes across the standalone AI liability market: **not disclosed** in the trade coverage I found.

## 4.3 What insiders are saying

- **George Lewin-Smith**, CEO, Testudo: "We are seeing more and more action on the exclusion side, whether it's in the E&O side or the GL side or the D&O side" ([The Insurer](https://www.theinsurer.com/program-manager/news/standalone-ai-liability-market-takes-shape-with-underwriting-discipline-key-to-2026-04-24/)).
- **Karthik Ramakrishnan**, CEO, Armilla: "The demand is going to be very, very strong" as adoption rates and public AI failures increase (same source).
- **Tim Zeilman**, HSB global head of product ownership: flags professional liability as the critical concern, particularly legal services where AI use without adequate human review has created malpractice exposure (same source).
- **The Insurer**'s own read on market conditions: Lloyd's capacity providers remain cautious, traditional carriers are uncomfortable holding emerging AI risk in legacy products, and MGAs need strong underwriting discipline and technical expertise to secure backing (same source). Note the direction: capital is available *for disciplined, audited, enterprise* risk.
- **Reshmi Suresh**, Head of Agentic Commerce, Worldpay (now Global Payments), on the consumer question: liability for "everything short of outright fraud" is unallocated ([Worldpay](https://www.worldpay.com/en/insights/articles/agentic-commerce-liability-is-still-being-written)).
- Underwriters are reportedly asking whether AI outputs are **reviewed by a human before reaching the public**, treating unreviewed outputs as higher risk ([Shumaker](https://www.shumaker.com/insight/the-new-ai-coverage-fight-exclusions-endorsements-and-denied-claims/); [Lathrop GPM](https://www.lathropgpm.com/insights/the-ai-coverage-gap-what-new-insurance-exclusions-mean-for-your-business/)). **Inference:** this is exactly the "ask before paying" fork on pamphlet page 6 — the market is already pricing human-in-the-loop, which validates that page.

## 4.4 Does it line up with our story?

**Supported:**
- The risk is real and the market agrees: purpose-built MGAs, Lloyd's capacity, a Munich Re subsidiary product, and new exclusions being written into standard forms all in the 18 months to mid-2026. Nobody in insurance will tell you this problem is imaginary.
- **Page 3 (keeping instructions beside the agent's actions) is the most valuable idea in the entire pamphlet set.** The market is converging on exactly this: certification standards and audit (AIUC-1), "authenticated purchase intent" (Amex), and underwriters asking about human review. A traceable instruction-versus-action record is the underwriting artefact this whole category needs and does not yet have in consumer contexts.
- **Page 6's control fork** — act within my rules vs ask before paying — maps directly to how underwriters are already differentiating risk.
- **Page 7's "You, the agent platform and the booking provider may disagree"** is precisely and independently corroborated by Worldpay: disputes involving agent misinterpretation, wrong purchases or out-of-scope action lack clear allocation between issuers, merchants and agent platforms. The pamphlet is right, and it is right for the right reason.

**Contradicted or complicated:**
- **The specific story on page 4 is the weakest possible example.** A US domestic airline ticket booked seven or more days out must be cancellable within 24 hours for a full refund with no penalty. If Alex's agent books a non-refundable fare and Alex notices within a day, there is no loss to insure. The pamphlet's "refund requested, request declined" beat requires either a foreign carrier, an OTA/third-party booking, a booking inside seven days of departure, or a delay of more than 24 hours in noticing. A knowledgeable audience will identify this in seconds. The honest version of the story is a **hotel or vacation-rental** non-refundable rate, or an OTA booking — and note that the OTA route is doubly useful because the DOT rule explicitly excludes third-party agents.
- **Amex has partly pre-empted the consumer proposition and did so in April 2026.** If the loss is on a card, from a registered agent, with authenticated intent, the network is promising to absorb it — for free, with no claim, no deductible and no policy. That kills the simplest version of a retail insurance product. It does not kill the concept, because Amex's protection is conditional on *registered* agents and *authenticated purchase intent*, undated, and unquantified. But the prototype must be positioned in the gap, not on top of it.
- **The industry is moving the other way on in-force cover.** Every consumer or small-business policy that might have silently responded is being endorsed to exclude generative AI, with agentic-specific exclusions under consideration. **Inference:** this creates the gap the concept fills *and* signals that carriers currently consider this risk something to shed rather than price — which is a hard starting position for a personal-lines pitch.
- **Nothing in the pamphlet's "help seeking a remedy" is insurance.** Pages 5 and 7 mostly describe a claims-advocacy and evidence-assembly service: trace instructions, pursue a refund, then check eligibility. That is valuable, but it is an assistance product, and assistance products are sold at low margin as policy add-ons. An insurance audience will ask whether the real business is the record-keeping layer (sellable to agent platforms and to underwriters as an underwriting input) rather than the indemnity.
- **Nobody sells this to consumers anywhere.** That is either the opportunity or the verdict. The absence of even a single personal-lines entrant, 18 months into a visibly hot commercial market with Lloyd's capacity available, is information. **Inference:** the likely reasons are that individual losses are small relative to claims-handling cost, and that the chargeback/network route is faster and cheaper for the customer.

**Unsolved by anyone:**
1. Whether an agent-initiated purchase the consumer generally authorised is "unauthorised" under Reg E / PSD2. Worldpay says neither regime addresses it. **Not found** either way.
2. Whether an instruction log is evidentially reliable — who holds it, whether it can be tampered with, and whether an insurer would accept it as proof of the scope of authority. This is the crux of the concept and nobody has solved it.
3. The allocation problem itself. Cards are building verification rails; no one has written the liability waterfall between consumer, agent platform, merchant and issuer.

## 4.5 What this means for a prototype

**Must demonstrate:**
1. **The instruction/action ledger as the product, shown as evidence.** Side-by-side: what the user authorised, verbatim and timestamped; what the agent did; where they diverged. Then show that artefact being used to (a) get a merchant reversal, (b) support a chargeback, and (c) support a claim — in that order of preference. The order matters enormously to an insurance audience: cheapest remedy first shows you understand claims economics.
2. **The waterfall, explicitly.** Merchant remedy → network/issuer protection (name Amex-style agent protection as a layer, not a competitor) → agent-platform responsibility → insurance as the residual. A prototype that positions insurance as the *last* layer is credible. One that positions it first is not.
3. **The pre-purchase control choice with a priced consequence.** "Act within my rules" versus "ask before paying" should visibly change limits, deductible or price. Underwriters are already asking about human review; showing you price it is the fastest route to their respect.

**Avoid claiming:** that this is a solved liability question; that a non-refundable US airfare booked a week out is uncoverable (it usually is not); that the insurer can compel a platform or merchant to accept fault; and any implication that cover applies regardless of whether the agent was "registered" or authenticated. Also avoid framing the loss as large — be specific that these are small-to-mid losses, which is what makes the claims-cost problem real and interesting.

## 4.6 What would change our mind

- A personal-lines carrier or MGA filing an actual consumer AI-agent-error endorsement, anywhere.
- Amex publishing **limits, exclusions and an effective date** for Agent Purchase Protection. Generous terms would largely close the consumer gap; narrow terms would open it wide.
- A regulator (CFPB, FCA, or an EU body) ruling on whether agent-initiated purchases are "authorised" for payment-protection purposes.
- Card network rules that explicitly allocate agent-misinterpretation loss — either to the platform (in which case insure platforms, not consumers) or to the consumer (in which case the concept has a market).
- Published loss data on the frequency and severity of consumer agent errors. I found none; without it, nobody can price this.

# 5. Understanding Longevity

> *A service comparing later-life annuities with accessible savings, to plan an income floor.*

## 5.1 Who is already doing this

**Bluntly: this is the most solved of the six. It is a well-populated, regulated, mature category with named incumbents on both sides of the Atlantic.**

**US — annuity comparison and purchase platforms:**
- **Blueprint Income** — direct-to-consumer marketplace for fixed, income and index annuities. **Acquired by Massachusetts Mutual Life Insurance on 8 February 2021** ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/112925-53)); it also underpins an AARP annuity distribution arrangement ([Retirement Income Journal](https://retirementincomejournal.com/article/with-blueprint-income-aarp-ups-its-ante-on-annuity-sales/)).
- **DPL Financial Partners** (founded by **David Lau**) — commission-free annuity marketplace for RIAs; acquired **AnnuityFix** and broker-dealer **Johnstone Brokerage Services in September 2023** to run a "breakaway accelerator" moving advisers' legacy annuity business to fee-based ([DPL](https://www.dplfp.com/about); [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/dpl-financial-partners)).
- **ImmediateAnnuities.com**, **Stan The Annuity Man**, **MyAnnuityStore**, **Fidelity** and others all publish live annuity quote comparison and QLAC-specific guidance ([Fidelity Viewpoints on QLACs](https://www.fidelity.com/viewpoints/retirement/QLAC-qualified-longevity-annuity-contract); [ImmediateAnnuities QLAC page](https://www.immediateannuities.com/qlac-qualified-longevity-annuity-contract/)).
- **BlackRock LifePath Paycheck** — the institutional version of exactly this idea: a target-date fund with an embedded lifetime-income option, made a plan QDIA in some plans. **Six plan sponsors live with nine more implementing as of December 2024**; **14 DC plan sponsors with a combined $27bn of TDF assets** had said they intended to offer it as of May 2024; described as available to about **half a million people** as of April 2024 ([PLANADVISER](https://www.planadviser.com/blackrock-makes-tdf-annuity-option-plan-qdia/); [BlackRock press release, 2024](https://ir.blackrock.com/news-and-events/press-releases/press-releases-details/2024/BlackRock-Activates-Retirement-Solution-Offering-A-Paycheck-For-Life/default.aspx)). Participant-level take-up rates: **not found**.

**The exact product the pamphlet describes — the later-life annuity — has a dedicated US tax wrapper that has been available for a decade.** A **QLAC** lets a person defer part of their RMD to as late as **age 85**. SECURE 2.0 removed the 25%-of-assets cap and raised the lifetime funding limit from **$125,000 to $200,000**, indexed — **$210,000 for 2026** ([Greenleaf Trust](https://greenleaftrust.com/missives/qualified-longevity-annuity-contracts-qlacs/); [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/viewpoints/retirement/QLAC-qualified-longevity-annuity-contract); [Annuity.org](https://www.annuity.org/annuities/qlac/)). The American Academy of Actuaries has published an issue brief specifically on **barriers to widespread QLAC adoption** and on improving market efficiency for these products ([AAA issue brief](https://actuary.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/IB.QLAC_.8.22.pdf)); the DOL has published on QLAC innovations and trends ([DOL/EBSA](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ebsa/researchers/analysis/retirement/innovations-and-trends-in-annuities.pdf)). Constraints cited: funds are locked with no lump-sum withdrawals, and even $210k may be small relative to large IRAs. **QLAC sales volume: not found.** I searched specifically and could not obtain a figure — that itself is a finding, and a good research commission.

**UK — the guidance and comparison layer is being built by the regulator, right now.**
- **Targeted support** is a new regulated category between generic guidance and full advice, created by the **FCA/HM Treasury Advice Guidance Boundary Review**. The FCA Board confirmed near-final rules as final on **26 February 2026**; permission applications opened **2 March 2026**; the regime **came into force 6 April 2026** ([FCA PS25/22](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps25-22-consumer-pensions-investment-decisions-rules-targeted-support); [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102mohl/targeted-support-goes-live-on-6-april-2026-what-you-need-to-know/); [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/risk-and-compliance/fca-unveils-near-final-rules-for-targeted-support-in-pensions-and-retail-investme-102lzft)). It lets firms make suggestions to *groups* of consumers with common characteristics — the FCA's own examples include suggesting an alternative sustainable withdrawal rate to someone drawing down unsustainably. The FCA estimates around **23 million** consumers are currently underserved by advice and guidance ([FCA AGBR](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/advice-guidance-boundary-review)). The FCA followed with **CP26/10** on simplifying pensions and investment advice rules ([Transact summary](https://www.transact-online.co.uk/adviser/adviser-support/technical-centre/fca-cp2610-simplifying-the-pensions-and-investment-advice-rules-what-you-need-to-know/)).
- **MoneyHelper / Pension Wise** provide free government guidance including annuity comparison. I did **not** verify the current state of the MoneyHelper annuity comparison tool; treat its existence as likely but unconfirmed here.

## 5.2 Traction, with evidence

- **US retail annuity sales hit a record $464.1bn in 2025**, a fourth consecutive record year ([LIMRA](https://www.limra.com/en/newsroom/news-releases/2026/limra-final-u.s.-retail-annuity-sales-set-new-sales-high-totaling-$464.1-billion-in-2025/)).
- **But the income-annuity slice is tiny and the deferred slice is shrinking**: **SPIA $14.4bn, up 6%**; **DIA $4.8bn, down 3%** in 2025. Fixed-rate deferred **$165.3bn (+6%)**; FIA **$127.9bn (+1%)**; indexed products together **45%** of total sales, up from 24% a decade earlier (same LIMRA release). Income annuities — the thing the pamphlet's "later-life annuity" actually is — are roughly **4%** of the US annuity market, and the deferred version is under **1.1%**. **My arithmetic on LIMRA's figures; flagged as inference.**
- **UK: record £7.4bn of individual annuity premiums in 2025 (+4%)**, the highest since pension freedoms — but only **87,600 annuities sold, down 2%**. Average value **£84,000 (+7%)**. Sales over £250,000 up **31%**, over £500,000 up **54%**. People aged 70+ buying up **8%**. **Escalating (inflation-linked) annuities rose 10% to just over 18,000**, the highest since 2013 ([ABI](https://www.abi.org.uk/news/news-articles/2025/2/another-post-pension-freedoms-record-for-annuity-sales/); [Professional Pensions](https://www.professionalpensions.com/news/4525576/record-breaking-annuities-larger-pots-drive-74bn-sales); [Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/larger-pension-pots-drive-record-2025-for-individual-annuity-premiums-abi/)).
- **Blueprint Income:** acquired by MassMutual Feb 2021.
- **DPL:** acquired AnnuityFix Sept 2023.
- **LifePath Paycheck:** 6 live sponsors + 9 implementing (Dec 2024); 14 sponsors / $27bn TDF assets intending (May 2024); ~500,000 people reachable (Apr 2024).
- **FCA targeted support:** live **6 April 2026**; **23m** underserved consumers.

## 5.3 What insiders are saying

- The **FCA**'s official position, in its own examples, is that the gap is *guidance at scale on decumulation decisions* — which is what the pamphlet proposes. That is an unusually strong regulatory tailwind, and it is a formal regime, not an opinion.
- The **American Academy of Actuaries** has documented barriers to QLAC adoption and approaches to improving market efficiency — i.e. the profession's own view is that the later-life annuity market is inefficient and under-adopted ([AAA](https://actuary.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/IB.QLAC_.8.22.pdf)).
- The **ABI** data read as an industry position: money is flowing in, engagement is not. Bigger pots, fewer contracts.
- The **academic "annuity puzzle"** literature — that voluntary annuitisation is far below what theory predicts — is the intellectual backdrop. I did **not** source a specific paper for this report; treat the general claim as well-known but uncited here.

## 5.4 Does it line up with our story?

**Supported:**
- The problem is real and officially recognised: 23 million underserved UK consumers, and a US market where income annuities are a rounding error inside a record-breaking annuity market.
- The pamphlet's **deliberate restraint is its greatest strength and its most commercially credible feature**. It says "a proposed comparison service for **existing** retirement-income contracts", "you could compare **actual contracts**", "you would need **actual prices and access terms** before choosing". That is a comparison-and-understanding service, not advice — which is precisely the space the FCA has just legislated into existence.
- Page 7's boundaries are genuinely good and genuinely unusual: an income floor does **not** cover care bills or medical expenses; **check whether the actual contract increases payments for inflation, and how**; check access rules, death benefits and provider obligations. The ABI's own data supports the inflation warning — only ~18,000 of 87,600 UK annuities sold in 2025 escalate, meaning roughly **four in five buyers bought a level income**. **My arithmetic on ABI figures; inference.**
- Separating "accessible savings" from "contract income under its own terms" (pages 3, 4, 6) is the correct and non-trivial conceptual move, and it is how practitioners actually construct a floor.

**Contradicted or complicated:**
- **This is a solved problem with named incumbents.** Blueprint Income (MassMutual), DPL, ImmediateAnnuities, Fidelity, plus the entire IFA and RIA channel, already compare actual contracts with actual prices. The pamphlet offers no mechanism that these do not have. Be honest with the team: as written, this reads less like a new product and more like a better-designed front end for an existing one.
- **Comparison is not the binding constraint; demand is.** UK annuity contract volumes *fell* in a record-premium year. US DIA sales *fell*. People are not failing to buy later-life annuities because they cannot compare them.
- **The unit economics are hostile.** Annuity distribution is funded by commission or by advice fees. A neutral comparison service that stops short of a recommendation captures neither and, in the US, cannot even present quotes without producer licensing and carrier appointments. **Inference**, but a confident one: this is why Blueprint Income ended up owned by a carrier.
- **"Robert is 61, hoping to retire at 65" is the wrong customer for this product.** A QLAC is an RMD-deferral instrument. The people for whom a later-life income floor is a live decision are typically at or past the point of drawing. Page 2's framing puts the service four years before the decision it is designed to support, which weakens the demonstration. **Inference.**
- **The word "annuity" is a marketing liability in both markets**, and the pamphlet uses it on four of eight pages. I did not source consumer-sentiment research on this; flagging it as a known practitioner belief rather than a found fact.

**Unsolved by anyone:**
1. Making unbiased decumulation comparison pay for itself without commission. FCA targeted support may change this in the UK from April 2026; nobody has yet proven a business model on it.
2. Getting people to act. Every institutional attempt — QLACs since 2014, LifePath Paycheck, the UK's post-freedoms market — shows supply and tax wrappers are not the bottleneck.
3. Comparing *access terms* rather than headline rate in a way consumers can use. This is genuinely under-served and is where the pamphlet is most original.

## 5.5 What this means for a prototype

**Must demonstrate:**
1. **A real contract comparison with real terms, side by side, including the ugly ones** — start date, escalation basis, surrender/commutation rights, death benefit, provider obligation. Comparing *access* and *inflation treatment* rather than headline income is the only differentiated thing here, and the ABI data (four in five buying level income) is your evidence that it matters.
2. **The gap visualisation.** Page 5's "start later, other resources bridge the gap" is the best idea in the pamphlet. Show the bridge explicitly: how much accessible savings must cover, for how long, and what happens if they run out first. That is a planning insight nobody's comparison table gives you.
3. **The regulatory posture, on screen.** Show where the service stops. In the UK, position it inside **targeted support** (live since 6 April 2026) and say so. In the US, show it as a licensed producer's comparison or a non-recommending information service. Get this wrong and an insurance audience dismisses it as unlicensed advice.

**Avoid claiming:** any recommendation, ranking or "best" contract; any illustrative income figure without a named provider and date; that the floor protects against care costs or inflation unless the compared contract says so; and any implication that the service is free of distribution economics.

## 5.6 What would change our mind

- Evidence that a **non-commission** decumulation comparison service has achieved scale and covered its costs — a UK firm authorised for targeted support, publishing engagement and conversion, would do it.
- QLAC sales data showing material growth post-SECURE 2.0. I could not find these figures at all; if they show a step change, the later-life annuity thesis strengthens considerably.
- Participant take-up data from LifePath Paycheck. High take-up when an annuity is the default would show the problem is framing, not demand — and would reframe this concept as a *defaults* product rather than a comparison product.
- Research showing consumers can and do act on access-terms comparison in a way they do not on rate comparison.

# 6. Price Certainty

> *An agreed annual net cost for a slice of household energy, settled both ways at year end, while keeping your existing variable-tariff supplier.*

## 6.1 Who is already doing this

**I could not find a single live consumer product matching this mechanism — a cash-settled annual net-cost agreement on household energy, sold separately from supply, while the customer keeps a variable tariff.** I searched for consumer energy hedging startups across the UK, EU and US, and for parametric or insurance-structured energy bill products. Everything I found was either (a) supply-side fixed tariffs, (b) government price caps, (c) utility budget billing, or (d) business/corporate hedging. If this product exists, I did not find it, and that absence is the single most important finding in this section. Treat it as a genuine white space *and* as a warning.

**What does exist, and why each is not the same thing:**

| Existing thing | What it does | Why it differs |
|---|---|---|
| **Utility budget / levelized billing** (US, near-universal) | Averages the last 12 months' usage into a flat monthly payment, with a **year-end true-up settling the difference both ways**. Enrolment is **free** ([NIPSCO](https://www.nipsco.com/bills-and-payments/billing-programs/levelized-billing); [OUC](https://www.ouc.com/solutions-programs/billing/budget-billing/); [NOVEC](https://www.novec.com/Customer_Services/Levelized_Billing.cfm)) | **Smooths cash flow; does not cap total cost.** The true-up passes price increases through in full. This is the crucial distinction the pamphlet must make — and the one an audience will miss, because the *surface mechanics* (agreed annual amount, settled both ways at year end) are nearly identical |
| **Fixed-rate retail supply contracts** (UK and US deregulated markets) | Locks the unit rate for a term | Requires changing or renegotiating supply. The pamphlet's explicit differentiator is keeping the existing supplier and variable tariff |
| **Ofgem price cap** (UK) | Caps the unit rate and standing charge on default tariffs; reset quarterly ([Ofgem, Apr–Jun 2026](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/news/changes-energy-price-cap-between-1-april-and-30-june-2026)) | A regulated ceiling on rates, not an agreed annual net cost, and it moves |
| **Norway's price schemes** | Households have received roughly **90% compensation for spot price above about 70 øre/kWh incl. VAT, capped at 5,000 kWh/month**; from **October 2025** an optional **"Norway Price"** fixed at **40 øre/kWh** ([Day Ahead Market](https://www.dayaheadmarket.eu/norway)) | This is the closest live analogue to the pamphlet's economics — and it is a **government** scheme, not a private product. Note it is *optional*, i.e. consumers choose certainty vs spot |
| **Octopus Energy Zero Bills** (UK) | Homes with heat pump, solar and battery get **no home energy bills for at least 5–10 years, guaranteed**. Over **5,000 homes** approved for eligibility with developers; target **100,000 by 2030**; 54 Barratt Redrow homes completing in 2026; 300-home Wisbech site from 2028; a global **Zero Bills Standard** ([Octopus press](https://octopus.energy/press/octopus-launches-global-zero-bills-standard-for-energy-bill-free-living/); [Barratt Redrow](https://www.barrattredrow.co.uk/news/2025/octopus-energy-and-barratt-redrow-team-up-to-wipe-out-home-energy-bills/)) | The most commercially successful "price certainty" product in the market — and it achieves certainty through **physical assets plus supply**, not a financial settlement. This is a direct strategic challenge to the concept |
| **Dynamic tariffs** (Tibber, Fortum Tarkka) | Pass through hourly spot plus a margin. Fortum is the largest Nordic retailer with **2.0m customers, ~12% share** ([Fortum](https://www.fortum.com/investors/why-invest-fortum/markets)); Tibber operates in Germany, Netherlands and the Nordics ([Fortum Tarkka](https://www.fortum.com/fi/sahkoa/en/electricity-for-home/tarkka)) | Moving in the **opposite direction** — the market's growth product is *more* exposure to price, not less |
| **Arbol** (parametric) | Custom indices on **Heating and Cooling Degree Days** and temperature thresholds to manage energy-demand exposure ([Arbol Energy](https://www.arbol.io/solutions/energy)) | **Business-facing.** Hedges volume/weather, not price, and not for households |
| **Sensible Weather** | The structural precedent worth studying: **Sensible Weather Insurance Agency LLC** is a nationally licensed insurance producer operating as an **MGA**, backed by **Sensible Weather Insurance Company**, a **Bermuda captive**, plus reinsurers; but **Weather Guarantees are marketed as warranties, not insurance products**, with automatic parametric reimbursement and no claims process ([Sensible Weather FAQ](https://www.sensibleweather.com/for-business/faq); [blog](https://www.sensibleweather.com/for-business/blog/weather-guarantees-cancelation-insurance)) | Not energy — but it is the best available template for how to deliver a parametric consumer promise without being a derivative |

## 6.2 Traction, with evidence

- Budget/levelized billing: free, ubiquitous, with annual true-up both ways. **Enrolment percentages: not found** — I searched and could not obtain penetration figures, which is a real gap for anyone sizing this concept.
- Octopus Zero Bills: **>5,000** homes approved eligible; **100,000 by 2030** target; **54** Barratt Redrow homes in 2026; **300** at Wisbech from 2028.
- Norway: **~90%** compensation above **~70 øre/kWh**, **5,000 kWh/month** cap; optional **40 øre/kWh** "Norway Price" from **October 2025**.
- Fortum: **2.0m** customers, **~12%** Nordic share.
- **Live consumer energy price-hedge products matching the concept: none found.**

## 6.3 What insiders are saying

This is the concept where I have the least insider material, and I want to be straight about that. I found no conference talks, trade-press commentary or named practitioner views specifically on selling cash-settled household energy price certainty. What I can report:

- **Worldpay's regulatory-gap framing has a direct analogue here** — but for energy I could not find an equivalent named practitioner. **Not found.**
- The structural constraint is in statute rather than in opinion. **Dodd-Frank amended CEA Section 2(e)**: "it shall be unlawful for any person, other than an eligible contract participant, to enter into a swap unless the swap is entered into on, or subject to the rules of, a board of trade designated as a contract market" ([CFTC OGC interpretation, Letter 12-17](https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/idc/groups/public/@lrlettergeneral/documents/letter/12-17.pdf); [7 USC §2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/7/2)). A household is not an eligible contract participant.
- **The CFTC's own carve-out is delivery-based, and that is the hinge.** The CFTC has clarified that typical retail energy contracts with homeowners are not subject to Dodd-Frank's retail commodity transaction requirements, and that agreements to purchase nonfinancial energy commodities for personal consumption are exempt **where the consumer takes delivery of and uses the fuel and the counterparty is a merchant delivering in the consumer's service area** ([Dodd-Frank.com summary of CFTC clarification](https://www.dodd-frank.com/2013/08/cftc-clarifies-that-typical-retail-energy-contracts-are-not-subject-to-regulation-under-dodd-franks-retail-commodity-transaction-provisions/); [CFTC fact sheet](https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/idc/groups/public/@newsroom/documents/file/fd_factsheet_final.pdf)). Every element of that exemption — delivery, personal consumption, merchant, service area — is a thing the pamphlet's structure deliberately removes by keeping the supplier separate.

## 6.4 Does it line up with our story?

**Supported:**
- The underlying customer problem is real, well-evidenced and politically salient: UK energy price volatility has driven a decade of parliamentary research and a statutory price cap ([House of Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-9714/)), and Norway pays households to smooth spot exposure. People do want predictable energy cost.
- **The pamphlet's disclosure discipline is outstanding and should be preserved.** Page 4 says "You accept $250 more in expected spending than the assumed $2,800. That difference is not an extra fee." Page 6 says the bill arrives first and "You would need enough money to cover the bill while you wait." Page 7 leads with "When prices fall, you could pay more." Very few real financial products are this honest about the cost of certainty and the working-capital requirement. This is the most defensible thing about the concept.
- Page 5's scope discipline — agreed usage, part of the rate, one year, renewal a separate choice, taxes and extra usage outside — is exactly the boundary-drawing an insurance or energy audience will look for.

**Contradicted or complicated:**
- **In the US, the mechanism as described is very likely an unlawful retail swap.** A cash-settled, bilateral, financially-settled contract on an energy price index, with a household counterparty, off-exchange, is the thing CEA §2(e) prohibits. The CFTC's consumer exemptions turn on the consumer taking *delivery* from a *merchant* in their *service area* — and the concept's defining feature is that delivery stays with a different party. **Inference from the statute and the CFTC's own guidance, not from a CFTC ruling on this exact structure.** But it is the first thing a general counsel will say, and the concept as drafted does not have an answer. In the UK/EU, the equivalent problem is that a cash-settled commodity contract for difference sold to retail consumers is a MiFID II investment product requiring FCA/NCA authorisation and appropriateness testing.
- **The mechanics are almost indistinguishable from free utility budget billing.** Agreed annual amount, pay bills as they come, settle the difference both ways at year end — that *is* levelized billing, and it costs nothing. The genuine difference (budget billing smooths timing and passes price through; this caps price level) is a subtlety that will be lost on most viewers and on some professionals. **The prototype's single hardest communication job is making that distinction unmissable.**
- **The winning product in this space achieves certainty physically, not financially.** Octopus Zero Bills delivers *actual* certainty — zero, guaranteed, 5–10 years — through heat pump, solar and battery, with developer distribution and a published standard. It is a materially better consumer offer than "we will settle the difference on part of your rate for one year". **Inference:** any insurance-shaped answer to household energy price risk now has to explain why it beats putting a battery on the roof.
- **The market's growth direction is the opposite one.** Tibber and Fortum Tarkka are growing by giving households *more* price exposure in exchange for the ability to shift load. The pamphlet sells certainty to a market currently buying optionality.
- **The one-year term makes the economics thin.** A one-year hedge on part of the rate on part of the usage, with an explicit $250 premium on $2,800 of expected cost, is about a **9% load** for a partial, short-dated hedge. **My arithmetic on the pamphlet's own numbers.** A consumer who understands that will often prefer a fixed tariff; a consumer who does not understand it is a mis-selling risk.

**Unsolved by anyone:**
1. A lawful retail structure. The best available template is **Sensible Weather's** — an MGA with a Bermuda captive, delivering a **parametric warranty** rather than insurance or a derivative. Whether that template extends from rainfall to an energy *price* index is the central open question, and I found nobody who has tried.
2. The float and counterparty problem. Who funds the year-end refund, and what happens if they fail after the customer has already paid a $4,100 bill?
3. Consumer comprehension of paying to give up upside. PPI's £38.3bn is the reminder of what happens when consumers do not understand what they bought.

## 6.5 What this means for a prototype

**Must demonstrate:**
1. **The difference from budget billing, in one screen, unmissably.** Two households, same year, prices rise 40%: one on budget billing (pays the increase, just later), one on this (net cost unchanged). If the prototype does not nail this, the concept reads as a paid version of something free.
2. **The legal wrapper, named.** Say whether it is a **parametric warranty** (Sensible Weather template), an **insurance** contract, or a **supply-side** product — and show the entity. In the US you must show why it is not an off-exchange retail swap. This is not a detail the audience will let you defer.
3. **The cash-flow and counterparty reality.** Page 6 already has the right instinct. Push it further: show the worst month, the working capital required, what happens at settlement, and who stands behind the refund. An insurance audience respects a product that shows its own failure modes.

**Avoid claiming:** that the product "fixes your energy bill" (it fixes a net cost on a slice); that there is "no fee" without the accompanying explanation that the target sits above expected cost; any settlement figure that is not clearly labelled illustrative; and any framing that positions it against a fixed tariff without acknowledging that a fixed tariff is simpler, cheaper to run, and already available.

**Consider testing the physical route as a comparator.** Putting Zero Bills-style asset-based certainty next to financial certainty in the prototype would be intellectually honest and would probably make the financial product look better in the cases where it genuinely wins — renters, existing homes, people who cannot install assets.

## 6.6 What would change our mind

- A **CFTC no-action letter, exemption or rule** permitting a retail cash-settled energy price hedge, or a named product operating lawfully in a US state.
- A **parametric energy-price warranty** launched by anyone, anywhere, structured on the Sensible Weather model — this would prove the legal template travels.
- Willingness-to-pay research showing households will pay a **~9% expected-cost premium** for partial, one-year price certainty. I found none; without it the pricing on page 3 is an assertion.
- Budget-billing enrolment and satisfaction data showing that households actively want *level* certainty rather than *smoothed* payments. Not found, and it is the cheapest useful study in this whole report.

# Cross-cutting 1: Regulatory reality

Jurisdiction is US unless stated. UK/EU noted where materially different.

## Micromutual

**US.** Two viable routes and one dead end.
- **Dead end:** an unlicensed entity that collects contributions and pays defined property losses under written rules with assessment and appeal is transacting insurance. Each state's unauthorised-insurer statutes apply. Guevara's death — unable to establish a capitalised underwriting vehicle ([Insurance Post](https://www.postonline.co.uk/insurer/3313886/start-up-guevara-closes-for-business-after-failing-to-establish-capitalised-underwriting-vehicle)) — is the template. The HCSM enforcement record shows what happens when a non-insurance pool uses insurance vocabulary and fails to pay: multi-state action, allegations about the proportion of fees reaching claims ([Georgetown CHIR](https://chir.georgetown.edu/aliera-healthcare-prompts-increased-state-activity-health-care-sharing-ministries/)).
- **Route A — community entity buys from a carrier.** The Isleton model: a **Geologic Hazard Abatement District**, a special district with power to assess fees and purchase private flood insurance on residents' behalf ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/catastrophe/california-launches-firstever-communitybased-flood-program-510367.aspx)). Or the NYC model: a nonprofit buys a parametric policy and distributes grants. Both are legal, both are proven, both require an institution that is not the neighbours.
- **Route B — reciprocal exchange.** Guy Carpenter is actively researching whether a **community-based parametric reciprocal exchange** can be authorised in California and Florida, and the research is explicitly about the regulatory environment ([Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/guy-carpenter-launches-new-parametric-solutions-research-project-for-california-florida/)). Reciprocals are a real, licensed US carrier form. The specific obstacle: subscriber capital, a licensed attorney-in-fact, and state admission — i.e. it is a carrier formation project, not a neighbourhood app.
- **Prevention-only pool.** If money is only ever spent on agreed physical work and never on indemnifying a member's loss, it is plausibly not insurance. **Inference** — get an opinion per state. Note the cleanest existing vehicles are an HOA assessment, a special district, or a Firewise USA community group.

**Discretionary mutuals** are a normal structure in Australia and, to a degree, the UK, where the promise is discretionary rather than contractual. I **could not find** authority for a consumer-facing discretionary mutual operating lawfully for property risk in a US state, and would assume it is not available absent specific advice.

**UK/EU.** A small mutual still needs PRA/FCA authorisation and Solvency UK capital. **Broodfonds's** answer — a *gift* arrangement, explicitly not insurance, no contractual right to payment, group capped at 50 ([Wikipedia: Broodfonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broodfonds)) — is the only structure I found that scales without authorisation, and it works because it forgoes enforceability.

## Emberwatch

- **Insurance side:** giving policyholders a free loss-prevention service is normal and long-established (Chubb, AmFam, USAA with WDS). Where it becomes regulated is **rating**: a premium credit must be filed. In California it must fit the **Safer from Wildfires** framework under 10 CCR §2644.9, which **AB 1** now requires to be updated regularly ([CDI](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0400-news/0102-alerts/2025/Commissioner-Lara-Announces-New-Wildfire.cfm)). Anti-rebating and inducement statutes are the other constraint on giving away valuable services — I did **not** research state-by-state rebating treatment of loss-mitigation services, and it is a real question for a prototype that bundles hardware.
- **Drones: this is the hard blocker.** FAA Part 107 plus a **Temporary Flight Restriction** over an active wildfire. Flying in a fire TFR risks a civil penalty up to **$75,000** and, for interfering with firefighting on public lands, up to **12 months** imprisonment ([NBC LA](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/california-wildfires/drone-collision-super-scooper-palisades-fire/3620389/)). During-event commercial UAS access effectively requires integration with the incident command structure. The specific obstacle is not paperwork; it is that the airspace belongs to the air attack.
- **Ground access:** California law since **2018** requires private firefighters entering an evacuation zone to check in with the local incident commander and follow instructions, including leaving when told ([CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/environment/wildfires/2025/01/private-firefighters-insurers/)).
- **Advice liability:** no licence needed to tell a policyholder to follow official instructions. But **inference:** voluntarily undertaking evacuation guidance plausibly creates a duty of care that did not exist, and the documented pattern is that insurers and agents have no duty to advise but are liable once they do and get it wrong ([Merlin Law Group](https://www.merlinlawgroup.com/liability-on-the-insurance-agent/)). I found **no** wildfire-specific case; this needs counsel.

## Employment insurance

- **US.** The mandatory **federal-state UI** system is a state monopoly for the statutory benefit; private cover is sold *on top*, and typically as **involuntary unemployment insurance** — historically a credit-insurance-adjacent line regulated under state credit insurance and A&H rules. The specific obstacles are: (1) **rate and form filing** in each state, and (2) reserving for a correlated, macro-driven risk, which is why capacity is scarce and why the market's largest product reached ~1,000 lives and stopped ([SterlingRisk](https://www.sterlingrisk.com/incomeassure-offers-private-solutions-to-reduced-unemployment-benefit-periods/)). Employer-funded supplemental unemployment benefit trusts exist under **IRC §501(c)(17)**; I did **not** verify current usage and flag it as a structure worth legal review rather than a finding.
- **Naming:** avoid "unemployment insurance" and "employment insurance". The latter is the name of Canada's state scheme. **Inference**, but the Aliera record shows regulators treat insurance vocabulary as material.
- **UK/EU.** The relevant obstacle is not authorisation but **conduct**: this is the product family that produced **£38.3bn** of PPI redress ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/the-ppi-scandal-is-far-from-over-heres-why-144688)). Expect FCA Consumer Duty scrutiny of fair value, target market, claims ratios and the sales journey. A bundled or incentivised sale is the specific thing to avoid.
- **Sweden** shows the lawful and commercially viable form: union/association group cover conditional on membership in the state **a-kassa** ([Unionen](https://www.unionen.se/nu/unionens-income-insurance)).

## Agent error protection

- **US.** Two routes. As **insurance**, it is a personal-lines filing — a homeowners/renters endorsement or a standalone monoline — and nobody has filed one that I could find. As a **service**, claims advocacy and evidence assembly is largely unregulated, though acting on a consumer's behalf against a merchant may touch state debt-collection or public-adjusting rules depending on design. **Inference.**
- **The prior-and-existing-remedy stack matters legally, not just commercially.** DOT's 24-hour rule (for direct carrier bookings ≥7 days out, **not** OTA/third-party bookings) ([DOT](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds)); card chargeback rights; and now **Amex Agent Purchase Protection** ([Amex](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/newsroom/articles/innovation/american-express-debuts-agentic-commerce-experiences--ace--devel.html)). An insurance product must be drafted to sit *excess* of these or it will pay for losses that were already recoverable.
- **The open regulatory question, stated by a payments insider:** neither **Regulation E** (US) nor **PSD2/SCA** (EU) contains agentic-commerce provisions, and liability for agent misinterpretation and out-of-scope action is unallocated ([Worldpay](https://www.worldpay.com/en/insights/articles/agentic-commerce-liability-is-still-being-written)). As of 2026 no government has enacted agentic-commerce regulation specifically assigning fault ([Chargeflow](https://www.chargeflow.io/blog/agentic-commerce-regulation-what-merchants-need-to-know) — SEO-adjacent source, consistent with Worldpay, treat as corroborating not primary).
- **Product-warranty vs liability regimes.** In the **EU**, the revised **Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853** brings software within product liability, while the proposed **AI Liability Directive was withdrawn by the Commission in 2025**. I am reporting both from background knowledge and **did not verify either in this research pass** — flag as unconfirmed and worth a dedicated check, because it materially changes whether an EU version of this concept is filling a gap or duplicating a statutory right.
- **The trend is exclusion, not inclusion.** ISO **CG 40 47 / CG 40 48 / CG 35 08**, effective January 2026, optional, remove generative-AI-arising liability from CGL and products/completed ops ([Independent Agent](https://www.independentagent.com/vu_resource/verisk-to-roll-out-new-general-liability-exclusions-for-generative-ai-exposures/)), with agentic-specific exclusions under consideration ([The Insurer, July 2026](https://www.theinsurer.com/ti/news/verisk-weighs-new-exclusions-for-agentic-ai-risks-2026-07-10/)).

## Longevity

- **US.** Comparing and presenting *actual* annuity contracts with *actual* prices requires a **state insurance producer licence** and carrier appointments; recommending one triggers the **NAIC Suitability in Annuity Transactions Model Regulation #275** best-interest standard as adopted state by state, and **Reg BI** if securities are involved. Holding out as providing investment advice for compensation triggers **RIA** registration. The specific obstacle for the pamphlet's design: a genuinely neutral, non-recommending comparison service has no commission and no advice fee, which is a business-model problem masquerading as a compliance one. **Inference on the economics; the licensing requirements are standard.** Note I did **not** verify the current state-adoption count for Model #275.
- **UK.** Materially different and materially more favourable. **Targeted support** went live **6 April 2026** under FCA **PS25/22**, creating a regulated category between guidance and advice, with permission applications open from 2 March 2026 ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps25-22-consumer-pensions-investment-decisions-rules-targeted-support); [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102mohl/targeted-support-goes-live-on-6-april-2026-what-you-need-to-know/)). **CP26/10** proposes simplifying the advice rules further. This is the only concept of the six with a brand-new regulatory regime built for it.

## Price certainty

- **US.** The specific obstacle is **CEA §2(e)**: a non-eligible-contract-participant may not enter a swap off a designated contract market ([CFTC Letter 12-17](https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/idc/groups/public/@lrlettergeneral/documents/letter/12-17.pdf)). The CFTC's consumer energy carve-outs are conditioned on the consumer **taking delivery** of the commodity for personal consumption from a **merchant** in their **service area** ([CFTC clarification summary](https://www.dodd-frank.com/2013/08/cftc-clarifies-that-typical-retail-energy-contracts-are-not-subject-to-regulation-under-dodd-franks-retail-commodity-transaction-provisions/)). The concept deliberately separates the financial settlement from delivery, which walks out of the exemption. Workarounds worth legal review: structure as **insurance or a parametric warranty** (the Sensible Weather MGA + Bermuda captive + warranty framing, [Sensible Weather](https://www.sensibleweather.com/for-business/faq)); or fold it into a **licensed retail supply** contract, which means becoming or partnering with a supplier and giving up the "keep your tariff" premise. State **PUC** rules govern the supply route.
- **UK/EU.** Cash-settled commodity contracts for difference sold to retail clients are **MiFID II** investment products: FCA authorisation, appropriateness assessment, and the existing retail CFD restrictions. Structuring as insurance moves it to PRA/FCA insurance authorisation instead. **Ofgem** licensing bites only if you are supplying energy — a purely financial settlement is not supply, which helps with Ofgem and hurts with the FCA. **Inference on the Ofgem point.**

# Cross-cutting 3: Other research worth commissioning

Ordered by how much I think it would change a build decision.

## Tier 1 — commission before building

1. **Consumer agent-error loss data.** Frequency and severity of AI agent purchase errors: how often, how much, and how often already recovered via merchant, chargeback or DOT rule. Nobody has this. Without it the Agent Error product cannot be priced and the claims-cost-to-loss ratio — which I suspect is the killer — cannot be assessed. Sources to try: card networks, chargeback vendors (Chargebacks911, Chargeflow), agent platforms, Worldpay/Global Payments.
2. **Claims-handling cost per claim, by concept.** All four indemnity concepts involve small losses: a $5,000 fence share, a several-hundred-dollar fare, a $650 energy settlement. Get real per-claim handling costs from a TPA and test whether any of these can be adjusted profitably. **This single number probably decides which of the six are viable**, and it is cheap to obtain.
3. **Legal opinions, three of them, jurisdiction-specific.** (a) Is a neighbourhood prevention-only pool transacting insurance in CA/TX/FL? (b) Can a household energy net-cost agreement be structured as a parametric warranty rather than a swap in the US, and as insurance rather than a CFD in the UK? (c) Is an agent-initiated purchase "authorised" under Reg E and PSD2? Each is a memo, not a study, and each can kill or unlock a prototype.
4. **Amex Agent Purchase Protection terms.** Not published as of the announcement. Get them — directly if possible. They determine whether the Agent Error consumer concept has a market or a competitor.
5. **Verisk/ISO agentic-AI exclusion drafting.** Verisk was weighing agentic-specific exclusions as of July 2026 ([The Insurer](https://www.theinsurer.com/ti/news/verisk-weighs-new-exclusions-for-agentic-ai-risks-2026-07-10/)). If personal-lines forms follow the commercial ones, the consumer gap widens dramatically. Track the filings.

## Tier 2 — commission alongside building

6. **Existing willingness-to-pay studies.** I looked for and did not find: WTP for partial one-year energy price certainty; WTP for insurer wildfire monitoring separable from free Watch Duty alerts; WTP for consumer AI-agent error cover. Before running new research, sweep for existing work: Ofgem/Citizens Advice consumer research, EPRI, ACEEE, LIMRA/SRI consumer surveys, Wharton Risk Center, and the FCA's own AGBR consumer testing (which will contain directly usable material on decumulation).
7. **Reinsurance appetite, by concept.** Concretely: will anyone reinsure (a) a geographically-concentrated small property pool, (b) consumer AI-agent error, (c) voluntary individual job loss? Stand already has **Arch and RenaissanceRe** behind wildfire-with-mitigation, which is a strong signal for Emberwatch. For the others, ask Guy Carpenter's parametric advisory team, Gallagher Re, and the Lloyd's Lab intake. The AI liability market's constraint per *The Insurer* is not demand but capacity providers' caution and the MGA's underwriting discipline — worth testing directly.
8. **Distribution economics.** For Employment, this is decisive: model the CAC for direct-to-consumer job-loss cover against the Swedish union-bundled channel and against employer-sponsored. **Inference**, but I would expect direct D2C to be an order of magnitude worse, and that is the real reason IncomeAssure stalled.
9. **Loss-data availability for mitigation credits.** The Insurance for Good work shows filed wildfire discounts ranging from under 1% to over 40% for the same measure, with thin evidence. Get the CDI rate filings and the actual filed credit tables for the top ten California writers. This is public and would let a prototype show *real* numbers instead of illustrative ones — a large credibility win.
10. **Adjacent consultations in flight.** Track: FCA **CP26/10** on simplifying advice rules; **AB 1**-mandated Safer from Wildfires updates and **SB 429** public wildfire cat model; Guy Carpenter's CA/FL reciprocal-exchange regulatory findings; EMVCo's agentic payments task force; and whether the CFPB takes a position on agent-initiated payments.

## Tier 3 — useful, lower urgency

11. **Broodfonds field research.** 677 funds, ~32,000 participants, 16 years of operating history, and governance rules refined by experience. Somebody should interview the operators about what actually breaks: free riding, group death spirals, exits, disputes. Directly applicable to Micromutual's pages 5 and 7.
12. **Post-mortem interviews.** Guevara's founders (Rich Philip, Mike Greer, Kim Miller, Paul Anderson) and whoever ran IncomeAssure at SterlingRisk. Two of the most informative failures in this set, and both are recent enough that the people are reachable.
13. **Anti-rebating and inducement law** for insurer-provided hardware and services, state by state. Relevant to Emberwatch bundling sprinklers and to Micromutual funding an arborist.
14. **Budget-billing penetration and satisfaction data.** Not found in this pass. Cheap, and it is the base rate against which Price Certainty must be judged.
15. **QLAC sales volumes post-SECURE 2.0.** Not found. LIMRA or the carriers would have it. It is the cleanest test of whether the later-life annuity has latent demand.
16. **Independent verification of mitigation effectiveness.** Frontline's 96% and Faura's 90–95% are company claims. An independent study of automated exterior sprinkler performance in the January 2025 fires would be valuable to the whole market, not just to us.
17. **The EU liability picture, properly.** Revised **PLD 2024/2853** scope for software, and the status and consequences of the withdrawn AI Liability Directive. I flagged both as unverified above; they should be verified before any EU-facing version of the Agent Error concept.
18. **Insurance-sector self-interest check.** Sector employment reportedly down 21% YoY with entry-level adjuster roles down 50% ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/breaking-news/insurance-keeps-losing-jobs--ai-is-only-part-of-the-story-589149.aspx)). If the Employment concept is shown to insurance audiences, expect their reactions to be about themselves. Worth designing the research protocol around.
