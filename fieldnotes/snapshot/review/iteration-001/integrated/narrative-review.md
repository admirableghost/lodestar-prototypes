## The walkthrough order and what each prototype is for

| # | Prototype | Screens | Role in the room |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Opening — The First Move | 2 | Establishes the present tense. |
| 02 | Home Again | 3 | Money → recovery. What can be committed in advance. |
| 03 | Negotiated Neighborhood | 3 | The first thing one household cannot buy alone. |
| 04 | Wildfire Protection | 6 | The business model: prevention as market creation. |
| 05 | Backed to Act | 3 | Insurance as an enabler of delegation to software. |
| 06 | OpenCover | 3 | Answers the question 01 asked. |

**The spine.** 01 and 06 are a deliberate bookend: 01 ends on "it can read
everything, it cannot sign", and 06 opens by granting exactly that permission.
That only pays off if 01 is walked first. 02 → 03 → 04 is a widening of scope —
one household, then one street, then a region — and 05 is the one change of
subject, from households to software.

## The one length problem

Wildfire Protection is the only prototype over three screens. Its causal spine
is 1 → 2 → 4 → 6. Screens 3 and 5 are elaboration: 3 makes coordination
tangible, 5 carries the evidence/moat argument. Cutting 3 is the cheaper cut
structurally and the more expensive one rhetorically, because it is the only
concrete demonstration in that deck. **Not changed this iteration** — it is a
product decision, not a QA finding.

## What distinguishes 03 from 04

They are the two that could be confused. They are not:

- **Scale.** The smallest object in 03 is one household and you can count all
  sixty. In 04 it is a settlement cluster in a region.
- **Subject.** 03 is a contract between neighbours and a carrier. 04 is a
  detection-to-suppression network and who pays for it.
- **Ink.** Green against cool graphite, so they do not read as one piece even
  side by side in a contact sheet.
