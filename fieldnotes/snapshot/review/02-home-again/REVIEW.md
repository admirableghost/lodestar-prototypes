## Concept

A rebuild arranged before the loss: what is committed in advance, what stays conditional, and why a bigger cheque cannot substitute for a builder.

## Screen by screen — the thought each screen is meant to land

**1. Arranged in advance**
The replacement plan for this house was specified, and the builder and recovery lead were held, while nothing was wrong.

**2. The promise**
Here is exactly what is committed, and — in a card of equal weight, not a footnote — exactly what stays conditional.

**3. Already decided**
The first days after. Five beats, only one of which is a claim.

## Key interactions

**As it stood / To current code / Code plus hardening**
Rewrites the fourth plan line and redraws the house — hardening adds sheathing, screened vents and a cleared edge.

**Show a regional event**
Fills every builder slot. One commitment degrades to "Falls back"; the other three are untouched. This is the argument that money does not shorten a queue.

**Replay from the start / Show all steps**
Drives the timeline and the ground through slab → scaffold → frame.

## What is factual and what is hypothetical

**Hypothetical**
- Household, region, builder, recovery lead and the eight-slot capacity meter are all invented. No sum of money appears anywhere.

Every screen states, once and plainly, that its content is invented. No real
insurer's premium, withdrawal, book or pricing decision is stated anywhere, and
no invented figure is attached to a real company's name.

## Known issues

- The eight-slot capacity meter is an invented quantity. It is marked hypothetical, but it is the one countable figure on the glass and the scarcity argument depends on it.
- Map pins are hidden on a phone wherever they would land under a plate, so the mobile ground is unlabelled in some states.
- Roughly the lower third of screens 1 and 2 at desktop is open paper. An elevation drawing is blank below its ground line; this is a composition call someone may want to overrule.

## Viewports tested

Tested against the **deployed** build before these screenshots were taken. The
harness asserts computed font-size, contrast against the composited rendered
background, touch-target size, clipping, overlap, horizontal overflow and
console errors — a layout audit that does not assert on computed font-size is
not a readability audit.

| **iPad landscape, browser chrome** | 1180 × 740 | pass |

| Desktop | 1920 × 1080, 1440 × 900, 1280 × 720 | pass |
| Mobile | 430 × 932, 390 × 844, 360 × 740 | pass — scrolls |

- Smallest rendered text anywhere: **11px** (uppercase eyebrow label).
- No text renders below 11px at any viewport. Running copy is ≥ 13px desktop, ≥ 14px phone.

- No state is carried by faded text.
