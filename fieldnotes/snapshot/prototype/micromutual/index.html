Prototype · 2036

Thirty-eight homes. One machine. One ledger.

Ten years after a carrier launched it, this is what a street-level pool looks like from the inside.

About this prototype

Everything here is a projection of 2036: the figures are invented, internally consistent and not measured, and no real insurer operates this.

01 / Who holds the money

Two of these are insurance. Neither is the pool.

Each of 38 households pays $86 a month into accounts that cannot lend to one another.

Held by
Tramonto Ridge Resilience Association, a California nonprofit corporation. Restricted account, custodian bank.
Authority
None required. Buys work; promises no payment.
The one rule
Out to a vendor invoice, for approved work on an enrolled parcel. Never to a member.
Held by
The same association, a separate restricted account. $6,100 a year in, three years' accumulation held.
Authority
State registration as a restricted community resilience fund. Examined and capped. Not insurance, and no enforceable promise to pay.
The one rule
$4,000 a household per event. Pro-rata when short. Silent on declared events.
Held by
A licensed carrier. Written to the association, not to a household.
Authority
An admitted carrier's filed form and rate. The insurance on this page.
The one rule
Attaching above $18,300. Annual aggregate. Premium $3,100.
Held by
Your own insurer, on your own terms, at your own deductible.
Authority
A homeowners contract. Where every large loss goes.
The one rule
Presented first, always. Never the same loss twice.

The pool never underwrites; the two enforceable promises on this page both belong to licensed carriers.

The one invented rule

That state registration is this projection's single piece of invented law — in 2026 no US state had authority for a fund that pays property losses without an insurance licence.

Not a mutual. Not an insurer.

04 / Two kinds of money

Clearing it is work. Replacing it is a claim.

An August windstorm drops a live oak limb across three properties and flattens a shed.

Works account

$3,850

  • Clearing, three parcels.
  • Vendor invoice, not a claim.
  • No deductible. No assessment.
  • Paid in nine days.

Hardship account

Bands showing your first $750, the pool's payment up to its $4,000 cap, and any remainder left with you. Above the policy's $10,000 deductible the policy responds and the pool pays nothing.

  • Household policy: $10,000 deductible.
  • Past it, the pool steps back.

The pool looks at a loss only after the policy has, and it never funds any part of a deductible.

Below that line it is capped at $4,000, and above the cap the money is yours — the pool does not make you whole, and it never says it will.

05 / Ten at once

Sized for ten.

Drag the number of households claiming in the same year.

Hardship layer

$0

  • By rule, not by shortfall.
  • A declared wildfire or windstorm — excluded in writing.

Your own policy

Responds

  • Its own limits. Its own deductible.
  • The only enforceable promise on the street.

What the pool bought

Prevention

  • 378 machine hours. 36 boundary gaps.
  • Modelled, not observed: a treated street, one third the structure-to-structure ignitions.

Ten households at the cap is exactly $40,000, which is the whole stack — the eleventh is paid out of the same money, so everybody's payment falls.

A wildfire is not ten households, which is why the hardship layer is silent on declared events and why most of the money sits in the works account instead.

06 / The carrier's side

What a carrier gets that it cannot buy.

Evidence

1,216

  • Parcel passes, eight years.
  • Before-and-after lidar on each.
  • Collected by the street, at the street's cost.

A designation to rate on

Enhanced

  • Wildfire Prepared Neighborhood.
  • Verified annually, parcel by parcel.
  • A community credit that is filed, not promised.

A layer to sell

14.3% rate on line

  • $21,700 limit, attaching at $18,300.
  • Premium $3,100. Annual aggregate.
  • Capped, and capped in writing.

A street that renews

38

  • Policies on one verified block.
  • No assessment power beneath them.
  • A retention story on a street that was being non-renewed.

The association buys the aggregate contract because it has no power to assess its members, so the shortfall is priced rather than argued about.

None of this promises a premium reduction — the community credit is filed, and in 2026 filed credits for the same measure ran from a fraction of a percent to over forty.

07 / Before you join

No assessments. No entitlement. No exit balance.

Three things a member is told before the first payment, and none of them sit in a footnote.

01

No assessments

  • No power to call for more money.
  • Short: less paid, never another invoice.

02

No enforceable promise

  • Published rules. Independent assessment. An appeal.
  • And no contractual right to payment.

03

Nothing to withdraw

  • Thirty days' notice, any time.
  • Contributions spent on work, not saved.
  • Your parcel record, yours to take.

The hardship layer works the way a Dutch bread fund works — it forgoes enforceability on purpose, because that is what keeps it out of the insurance code.

08 / Still unsolved

Four things ten years did not solve.

Everything before this page is a projection; these four are the parts a projection cannot honestly close.

  1. Willingness to pay

    • $86 a month, against a credit that may be worth a fraction of a percent.
    • Never demonstrated at scale, by anyone, anywhere.

  2. The authority

    • The one invented rule on page two.
    • In 2026: no state, no precedent.
    • An unlicensed pool paying property losses is transacting insurance.

  3. Consent

    • A geofence enforces a refusal.
    • It does not change a mind.
    • One adjacent pair still caps the street.

  4. Correlation

    • Sized for ten. A fire is not ten.
    • The pool's answer to the common peril is prevention, not payment.
