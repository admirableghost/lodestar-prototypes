## Why this photo (M27 lane D)

The M25 hero (`julian-hillside-home-source.jpg`, a mountain-Victorian with a turret in Julian, San Diego County backcountry) was visibly not a Pacific Palisades home: the architecture said mountain town, not coastal canyon. The M27 brief for the replacement: a contemporary Southern California hillside home that is plausible for the Santa Monica Mountains or a coastal canyon — stucco or modern, dry chaparral and hillside context — no fire or smoke, no people, no visible street number, ≥ 1600 px wide, freely licensed (Unsplash, Pexels or Wikimedia Commons CC0/CC-BY).

This frame: a two-storey white stucco home with a terracotta tile roof and chimneys, sitting on the brow of a slope with a wooden deck on stilts below it, eucalyptus and a palm beside it, dry scrub and yellow-flowering brush across the whole lower frame, and a hazy ridge and town behind. Stucco-and-tile on a slope with a stilted deck is the Palisades / Malibu canyon vernacular; the vegetation is dry coastal scrub, the fuel the story is about. The house reads as someone's home, not a listing. The light is warm late-day; the shared CSS dusk grade (below) does the rest.

Checked at full resolution (5178×2913): no people, no street numerals, no signage or brand marks, no fire or smoke. A faint bird in the sky at upper right; a neighbour's eave touches the source's left edge (x 0–3 %) and a small shed roof sits below the house. Both edge details are dropped or subordinate under the crop and the vignette.

Compromises, stated plainly: the photograph is San Diego, not Pacific Palisades (a coastal Southern California hillside, ~200 km south of the demonstration property); the caption on stage stays "This is the home he's built his life around" and the location line beside the household mark is `HOUSEHOLD.location`, so the photograph is never labelled as the Palisades property; it is a daylight frame graded to dusk in CSS, not a dusk exposure.

## Required treatment (binding)

1. **Crop.** In `frontend/src/components/frames/Opening.css` the cover-fit image is scaled slightly past fit (`--opening-photo-scale`, `--opening-photo-origin`) from an origin left of centre, which drops the neighbour's eave at the source's left edge at 1440×900 and 1024×768 and keeps the house centre-left under the leader from the protection column. `.opening__photo` clips the scaled image so the grade and vignette always cover everything seen (this matters on narrow panels, where the photograph is a block at the top of a scrolling column). No street numerals exist in the frame (checked at full resolution).
2. **Day-for-dusk grade, live in CSS.** Multiply dusk gradient, a still "someone is home" light on the upper-floor windows, vignette and scrims — all mixed from design tokens. The file itself is unaltered.
3. **Never caption it as the real demonstration property** or as "Jeff's actual house"; copy stays at "This is the home he's built his life around."

## What this photograph is, and what it is not

**This is a real person — a photographer's subject — standing in for a fictional customer.** He is not Jeff, there is no Jeff, and this image must never be captioned, labelled or implied to be a real policyholder of any insurer. On stage he is only ever named as `HOUSEHOLD.displayName` ("Jeff V."), a fictional identity from `frontend/src/content/household.ts`, and every surface that names him also carries `ILLUSTRATIVE_NOTE` once. Do not attach his real name or the photograph's own title to him anywhere in the product.

This environment cannot generate images, so a sourced photograph was the only way to let the audience meet a person. The constraints applied when choosing it:

- an adult man who reads as roughly 35–60, at or near a home — a doorway, porch or driveway — environmental rather than a headshot;
- documentary, natural styling; not smiling-at-camera stock energy; partly turned rather than posed square-on;
- no children, no visible names, street numbers or brand marks, no celebrity likeness;
- Unsplash or Pexels only, ≤ 2 MB.

## Why this one

About forty candidates were reviewed across both libraries (searches: man on porch / doorway / driveway / front yard / garden gate / homeowner, plus the Pexels "more like this" series around this shoot). Most failed one of the constraints: the Unsplash porch series by Samuel Yongbo Kwon shows a man in his twenties from behind with the house number "1007" in frame; the GN Group "man in blue jacket" is a hearing-aid brand campaign whose subject is named on the page and is holding architect's plans; the RDNE Spanish-bungalow frames hide the face behind sunglasses and read as staged stock; the Kindel Media frames of a second man at a picket gate (8172239–8172249) are captioned "distressed" / "unhappy" and show the street number "1035"; the remainder were the wrong age, the wrong geography (cabins, Cuba, Kurdistan, rural Turkey), delivery couriers or realtors.

This frame was chosen because he looks like someone who lives here: a man in his fifties in a short-sleeved plaid shirt, one hand resting on the frame of his own doorway, a jade plant on the sill, a screen curtain behind him, looking out with a calm, slightly guarded expression. The frame contains no numerals, signage or logos (checked at full resolution).

Known compromises, stated plainly: it is a stock-studio photograph (Kindel Media shoots for Pexels), so the same face may appear in other people's marketing; the stucco behind him is cool blue-grey while the hillside home is warm white stucco, which the shared CSS dusk grade narrows but does not erase; and he looks toward the camera rather than at the home.

## Where the treatment lives

- `frontend/src/components/frames/Opening.css` — the `--opening-jeff-*` custom properties: a 4:5 window, cover-fit with the focal point on the face, under the same multiply dusk gradient and vignette as the house photograph so the two read as one evening. Since M27 the window is part of the caption block in the composition's lower-left — beside "This is Jeff.", its base on the caption's baseline — rather than a floating inset; it arrives with the line and stays.
- `frontend/src/components/HouseholdMark.css` — the `--hm-photo-*` custom properties: the circular crop for the identity lockup is a CSS window onto the same file (width / offset of the image inside a `--control-height-*` circle), so the source stays a single unaltered file. `HouseholdMark` renders the photograph only for the hero household; every other household gets a monogram, never an invented portrait.
- `frontend/src/components/frames/Portfolio.css` — the hero's map mark uses the same window (`.portfolio__home-img`).
