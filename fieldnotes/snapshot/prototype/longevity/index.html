This is an invented projection of 2036, ten years after a carrier ships the service; every figure, contract and household on these screens is made up, nothing is measured, and no real product is quoted. It compares and explains, which is guidance and not financial advice, and it promises no return to anyone.

01 / The position

Nothing here was typed in.

Robert is 71 and Ada is 68, and the household agent has held their real position continuously since the year this service shipped, so the comparison starts from what is true rather than from what someone remembered on a form. Their floor — the part of a month that is not optional — is measured from what they actually spend, not estimated from a bracket.

Each figure names its source and the moment it was last reconciled, and any one of them can be challenged and corrected by the household.

02 / The terms

The rate is the last row.

Four terms decide how this ends — what happens to inflation, what happens to access, what happens to Ada, and who stands behind the promise — and the headline income is none of them. Open any cell to read what that contract actually says.

03 / Consequences

Run a life, not a quote.

The same £150,000 goes into each contract, and every candidate is run against a whole life rather than priced at a moment. Choose a life: the order changes, and the contract with the highest first payment is not the one that carries the household furthest.

04 / The bridge

Waiting is not free.

A deferred contract pays nothing at all until it starts, so accessible savings carry the whole household across the gap alone. Drag the start date: every year of waiting buys a larger payment and leaves a longer, thinner bridge to cross before it arrives.

05 / Irreversibility

What you cannot undo.

Thirty days after the money moves, almost nothing about this decision can be reversed at any price, and that fact is nowhere on a rate table. Move the marker to a date and read what is still open on it.

06 / The boundary

Where this stops.

No recommendation

It narrows, compares and explains, and it never ranks the three, names a best one or tells Robert which to sign.

Targeted support, UK

The FCA regime live since April 2026 permits suggestions to groups of consumers who share characteristics, which is the whole of what runs here, and it stops deliberately short of a personal recommendation.

A licensed producer, US

The same screens are a producer's comparison rather than a proposal, and the moment any of it became a recommendation the best-interest standard would attach to it.

The handover

Press hand over and a regulated human receives the whole file, not a lead.

  • The position, with every source and reconciliation time
  • Every term opened, and when it was opened
  • The four lives run, and the assumptions behind each
  • The start age chosen, and the bridge it implies
  • What was never asked: tax, estate, property, health
  • A record that no recommendation was made here

Understanding Longevity

Ten years in.

Rate comparison was solved, well funded and busy long before this shipped, and on the evidence it was never the thing standing between a household and a decision. On 2025 ABI figures roughly four in five UK annuity buyers took an income that never rises, which remains the strongest argument there is for putting escalation, access and survivorship ahead of the headline.

What 2036 changed

  • Continuous household finances, so no intake
  • Cheap compute, so a life instead of a quote
  • Household machines, so a cheap long middle of care and an acute end that still is not

What it did not change

  • Annuity capacity, never scarce
  • Rate transparency, already everywhere
  • The irreversibility, exactly as irreversible

What a carrier gets

  • Not a better quote engine
  • A household that understood what it bought
  • A file that shows it did, and a boundary drawn on screen
