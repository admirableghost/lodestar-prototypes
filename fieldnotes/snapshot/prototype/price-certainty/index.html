Price Certainty · Edition 06 · Interactive

A steadier cost,ten years on.

In 2036 nobody reads a tariff: a household sets its comfort bounds once and an agent trades the home's flexibility against the grid for the rest of the year, and a carrier turns the proceeds into one annual number the household can plan around. This prototype walks that product from the household's side through to the underwriter's, and then keeps going until it breaks.

A projection of 2036 with invented figures, not a forecast — and no real utility, retailer, insurer or reinsurer shown here is a participant.

01 / What changed

Nobody reads a tariff any more.

The household states its bounds once, and after that its agent buys, sells, shifts and stores against a half-hourly grid signal without asking again. That is what makes the rest of this possible: the home now owns something a carrier wants, and certainty can be bought with it rather than with money.

      02 / Who is in the room

      Flexibility is the new capital.

      Everything a carrier can sell on a household's behalf depends on hardware that household can host, so the homes most exposed to a volatile market have the least to trade. Choose one; it stays chosen for the rest of the prototype.

        05 / The months you lose

        Some months you pay to be wrong.

        Every month settles against the flat annual target, so a mild month is a month the household hands money back for certainty it did not end up needing. Across a calm 2036 the chosen home gives up more than it gets and the product is still working exactly as sold.

        Bill against target, and the settlement it creates

        06 / The thing it is not

        Budget billing is free, and it does not do this.

        Levelized billing averages last year's usage into a flat monthly payment and trues up the difference at year end, which looks identical from the outside and passes every price rise through in full. Certainty changes the total; smoothing only changes when you pay it.

        Same home, same energy, same year — three arrangements

        07 / Where the swing lands

        Someone is short this.

        Push market prices up across the whole book and the shortfall climbs three layers in order — the flexibility revenue the pool earns, the captive's own capital, then the reinsurance tower — because the risk load is already priced inside every household's target. The figure under the handle is the point at which the tower is gone and the warranty pays pro rata, which is the honest description of what a household is actually holding.

        08 / Cash, not just price

        The bill still arrives first.

        A home facing a — February against a — monthly target must find the difference before any settlement reaches it, and the household least able to float that is the one this product is for. Continuous settlement is what 2036 fixes: the carrier settles weekly, so the float never outgrows a few days.

        And when it does not work

          09 / The wrapper

          Two structures, two regulators.

          Settlement-only leaves the supplier untouched and, in the United States, looks very like an off-exchange swap sold to someone who cannot lawfully enter one. Supply-coupled gives that premise up: the carrier delivers the energy it dispatches, so the settlement rides on a supply relationship — the test the consumer carve-out turns on, and one nobody has put to a regulator.

          10 / The comparator

          Or just put a battery on the roof.

          The most successful version of price certainty settled nothing: it gave homes a heat pump, solar and a battery, and guaranteed no bill for years. For two of these three the hardware wins outright, and the financial product only has a market where hardware cannot go.

          11 / What would have to be true

          A subsidy with a market attached.

          This holds only while asset-rich homes stay in a pool that buys their flexibility for less than it is worth to them, because that spread is the entire funding for the homes with nothing to assign. Nothing compels them to stay. If they do, it is the rare financial product that hands certainty to people who cannot buy it.
