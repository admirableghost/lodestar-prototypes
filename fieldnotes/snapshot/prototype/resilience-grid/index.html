The discount gap

22 of the top 25 US property insurers already offer a wildfire mitigation discount.

It averages about $100 a year — roughly a tenth of the annualised cost of the work. Three insurers removed wildfire from rating altogether rather than pay the credit, affecting over 500,000 policies. Resources for the Future, 2025.

01 / The network

Every layer exists.The network does not.

Forty-three companies across twelve countries already build the sensing, verification and suppression layers. None of them sells the assembly. An insurer that helped assemble it would be paying to change the risk instead of paying to describe it.

The point

All four layers already have sellers. Not one of them sells the assembly.

Switch a layer off to see what the rest of it is worth without it. No participant here can build it alone, and no participant here can fund it alone.

04 / The loop

The drone isthe easy part.Proving it workedis the business.

The operating loop

01 Observe

Three independent sources, one ignition. Cheap, and getting cheaper.

02 Intervene

De‑energise, cut fuel, put water on the head of it. Purchasable today.

03 Verify

Prove the work happened: which parcel, to what standard, on what date.

04 Measure

Prove the loss that did not occur. Every number anyone has for this is modelled, not observed.

05 Update

Feed the result into the rate, the renewal, and next season's work.

Observe and intervene are procurement; anyone can buy them. The loop fails at 03 and 04, never at 02.

Where an edge might sit

The sensors and the aircraft are for sale to everyone. Across seventeen insurer prevention deals on record, not one gives the insurer exclusive rights to the data.

Strongest
Intervention‑and‑outcome logs the insurer owns, joined to its own claims book
Maybe
Co‑ownership of the verification standard — if anyone writes one
Maybe
Being the party that convenes the payers. A position, not a technology
Weakest
Priority access to aircraft, and the partner list itself

Swiss Re bought Fathom outright rather than partner for its flood models. Ownership, not access, was the route to exclusivity. This is reasoning, not a finding.

When prevention fails

Baloise funded hail cloud‑seeding flights with ETH Zurich from 2018 to 2023. It ended them, saying the effect on large hail "has not been demonstrably measurable." Announced October 2024.

The flights were real. Measurement is what failed — and that is what ended the funding. An effect nobody can measure is an effect nobody funds twice.
