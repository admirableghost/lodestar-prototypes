The discount gap

22 of the top 25 US property insurers already offer a wildfire mitigation discount.

It averages about $100 a year — roughly a tenth of the annualised cost of the work. Three insurers removed wildfire from rating altogether rather than pay the credit, affecting over 500,000 policies. Resources for the Future, 2025.

01 / The network

The pieces exist.The agreementis the proposition.

Forty-three companies across twelve countries build these layers, and utilities, fire agencies and insurers already buy from them. What does not arise on its own is a durable operating and economic arrangement between parties whose incentives are currently fragmented.

What is actually missing

These layers already meet at incidents. What is missing is the standing arrangement between them.

Who commissions the work, who verifies it independently, who pays for which part, and what evidence would change an underwriting decision. That institutional layer is what Resilience Grid is testing — not the hardware.

What the agreement does

A regional agreement commissions delivery and independent verification; carriers define what evidence could support coverage capacity and terms.

Build
Little or nothing. The hardware is commodity and purchasable by anyone
Partner
Grid action, dispatch and command, permits, capital structure
Verify
Independently, by a party that is neither the payer nor the beneficiary
Consume
Verified outcomes, as an input to risk estimates
Underwrite
Translate that evidence into capacity and terms, where it holds up

04 / The loop

The drone isthe easy part.Showing it workedis the business.

The operating loop

01 Observe

Three independent sources, one ignition. Cheap, and getting cheaper.

02 Intervene

De‑energise, cut fuel, put water on the head of it. Purchasable today.

03 Verify

Independently record what work happened: which parcel, to what standard, on what date.

04 Measure

Estimate risk reduction against a credible comparison, and carry the uncertainty with it.

05 Learn & update

Revise which interventions are worth repeating, and what they imply for risk and coverage.

Observe and intervene are procurement; anyone can buy them. The loop gets hard at 03 and 04 — and no step here delivers clean causal attribution.

Where an edge might sit · hypothesis

The sensors and the aircraft are for sale to everyone, and across seventeen insurer prevention deals on record not one secured exclusive rights to the data. So the candidate is not the data itself.

The claim
Becoming the carrier that translates independently verified prevention outcomes into underwriting capacity and terms
What compounds
Operating history connecting intervention to outcome to underwriting — if it is long enough to beat buying the same feed
Also possible
Being the party that convenes the payers. A position, not a technology
Not an edge
Commodity sensing, vendor data, priority access to aircraft, the partner list

Every line above is a hypothesis. We found no insurer doing any of it, and a mandate like Colorado's could make the translation step table stakes rather than an advantage.

When prevention fails

Baloise funded hail cloud‑seeding flights with ETH Zurich from 2018 to 2023. It ended them, saying the effect on large hail "has not been demonstrably measurable." Announced October 2024.

The flights were real. Measurement is what failed — and that is what ended the funding. An effect nobody can measure is an effect nobody funds twice.

The question to answer first

What incremental protection does the network provide beyond proven property hardening and fuel management?

The best‑measured prevention evidence anyone has is structural: IBHS FORTIFIED homes in Hurricane Sally saw 55–74% fewer claims and 15–40% lower severity. Study cited 2026. Harder interventions may be harder to prove.

05 / Market creation

The product is notthe drone. It is a marketthat might stay open.

Without the network the chain runs one way, and the household is standing at the end of it. Prevention does not create availability on its own: the chain only breaks if the intervention changes risk, the change can be credibly estimated, and capital accepts the remaining tail.

Without the network

Risk rises

Price rises to match

Availability falls

The carrier retreats

The household holds the risk

With the network · each step conditional

Outcomes observed and independently verified

Risk estimates improve, with uncertainty

Carrier and reinsurer reassess tail risk and capital

Terms and capacity could be revised

Cover could expand, or remain available

Each step on the right is a condition, not a consequence: if intervention materially changes risk, and that change can be credibly measured, and capital providers accept the resulting tail, then coverage may become economically viable.
