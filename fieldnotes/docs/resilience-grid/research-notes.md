# Resilience Grid — evidence base

Condensed from **"Backing pack: Seneca's space, and prevention businesses that
rhyme with it (2026-10-05)"**, prepared for Jonathan Matus by Asad Hussain
(Alliance Partners). Source numbering below is that document's own numbering
(`^n^` in its brief, `G1`/`G2`/`G3` in its fact sheets, `W1`/`W2`/`AS-*` in its
evidence rows). Nothing here was re-verified against the primary URLs in this
session; it is verified *against the pack*, which itself records a fact-check
pass (Part 4) with 176 of 220 claims confirmed at source.

This file exists so the prototype can be held to it. **Every number and every
named company on screen must appear in §1 or §2 below.** Anything in §3 or §4
may inform the concept but may not be stated as fact on screen.

## 0. The three tiers, and how they must be marked on screen

The pack is scrupulous about this and the prototype must be too.

| Tier | Pack's label | How the prototype must show it |
|---|---|---|
| **Verified** | `verified-primary` / `verified-press` | Nameable, quotable, dated. Lives only in the "Signals already here" plate on screen 6, and in two or three in-line asides. |
| **Inferred** | `inferred`, or "This is our reasoning, not something we found in use" | May appear as an argument, never as a finding. Hedged verbs only: *could*, *might*, *would*. |
| **Concept** | `concept`, "we found no company" | Future-scenario only. Must sit inside the 2036 frame, labelled. |

The pack's own closing sentence on most cards is the model for our tone:
> "This is our reasoning, not something we found in use."

## 1. Verified — the wildfire ecosystem is already fragmented, and large

fact sheet G1). The countries: United States, Argentina, Australia, Canada,
Finland, Germany, Greece, Israel, Netherlands, Poland, South Korea, Spain.

The eight groups, which are exactly the "see / understand / act" layers the
prototype needs — this is the single most load-bearing finding for the concept,
because it means **the network already exists as parts and has never been
assembled**:

Two further verified facts about the buyers, which matter to the business model:

- **The Aspen Fire Protection District buys from three of these companies at
  once** — Seneca (suppression), Pano AI (cameras), OroraTech (satellite) — and
  the Seneca and OroraTech purchases are funded with **foundation and donor
  money**^3,29^ (G1 "Other flags"). A fire district is already assembling a
  mini-network, and is already not paying for it alone.
- **Utilities are already the largest payer for ignition prevention.** PG&E
  filed a 10-year, 5,000-mile undergrounding plan (AS-DR1-051); SCE runs 650+
  cameras; Xcel had 218 AI cameras and 207 weather stations across eight states
  as of 2025 (AS-DR1-025). California's inverse-condemnation framework is why:
  PG&E's own investor presentation language is that utilities act as
  "uncompensated insurers of last resort" (AS-DR1-056).

**The load-bearing negative finding, verbatim from the pack's summary:**

> "None of the insurer prevention deals we found gives the insurer exclusive
> rights to the data. That raises the question of how long an early partner's

And the pack's own count: of 17 deals, "Four tie the price or availability of
cover to verified prevention. They are Stand, CitroTech, FortressFire and the
Tahoe Donner policy. None we read gives the insurer exclusive rights to the

Swiss Re took the other route and **bought the flood-model company Fathom
outright**^16^ — i.e. the way to make data exclusive was to own the company,
not to partner.

### Prevention paid for out of insurance capital structure — the NCIUA bond

The strongest precedent in the pack for "avoided-loss economics can finance
mitigation":

- The North Carolina Insurance Underwriting Association's **$600 million**
  Cape Lookout Re catastrophe bond carries an **extra 0.35% "resilience"
  spread** that comes back to the association to fund **IBHS FORTIFIED**
  storm-resistant roofs **if losses stay below a set level** — specifically,
  below 60% of the attachment point^21,22^.
- **Hannover Re fronted it**, as it has on all seven NCIUA cat bonds since
  2019^21^.
- A **second bond in 2026** repeats the feature (upsized $600M Cape Lookout Re
  2026-1)^23^.

Note the conditionality carefully: NCIUA *pays* the extra spread, and the money
only returns to mitigation if the bond does not take losses. It is not free
money; it is a bet that prevention pays for itself.

### What FORTIFIED is worth, measured

A peer-reviewed study of IBHS FORTIFIED homes in Hurricane Sally found
**55–74% fewer claims and 15–40% lower claim severity**, which would have let
insurers avoid an estimated **$105–116 million** in losses (pack G3 K07/X4 via
UBS, 2026-03-16). This is the clearest measured avoided-loss number in the
whole pack — and it is *construction standards*, not sensing or suppression.

### Prevention can fail — the Baloise case

> Baloise ran hail cloud-seeding flights with ETH Zurich from **2018 to 2023**,
> then said in **October 2024** that the effect on large hail "**has not been
> demonstrably measurable**."^28^

Flights ended 2023; the end was announced Oct 2024. A separate Arizona State
study of a Kansas county cloud-seeding programme found it reduced hailstone
size but "did not significantly decrease crop damage from hail or drought"
(AS-TL3-007).

**This is the most strategically important single fact in the pack for this
prototype.** It is the reason the prototype's centre of gravity must be the
*verification* step, not the drone.

### Insurability already fails for other asset classes

The pack's cross-cutting finding X1 ("operators of high-value assets
self-insure or drop cover"), all `verified-primary` from filings:

- **KLA** self-insures California earthquake risk: "We self-insure earthquake
  risks because we believe this is a prudent financial decision based on our
  cash reserves and the **high cost and limited coverage available** in the
  earthquake insurance market."^11^
- **Telesat** insures only one of its geostationary satellites^12^.
- **Iridium** carries no in-orbit insurance and does not expect to obtain it^13^.
- **EchoStar's** satellite unit generally carries none, judging the cost
  uneconomic^14^.
- **SpaceX**: "We do not typically obtain insurance coverage for our satellites…
  we bear the full financial cost of any such losses."^114^

X1's own caveat, which the prototype must respect: this fits **single sites and
geostationary satellites**, and fits large constellations *less*, because
Iridium and SpaceX rely on in-orbit spares rather than insurance. Prevention
restores insurability where the asset cannot be made redundant — a house
cannot.

### And insurers are already retreating from wildfire-exposed property

- **Progressive** reduced its weather-exposed share of insured value by 23% via
  targeted non-renewals, and said in Aug 2026 the work was "substantially
  complete" and it is now expanding *slowly* in higher weather-risk states^17,18,19^.
- Expert-call evidence: major national carriers have "tightened their
  eligibility guidelines and chosen to pass high-risk properties on to the
  state-backed California FAIR Plan," creating "a significant opening for
  agile, tech-driven specialty carriers" (AS-DR2-073).
- An analyst note on Honeycomb warns the other way: a concentrated book means
  "a single severe weather season or regional wildfire event can simultaneously
  stress their loss ratios, trigger reinsurer scrutiny, and damage their
  underwriting credibility"^20^.
- Regulatory pull exists: California's Sustainable Insurance Strategy lets
  insurers put catastrophe modelling and reinsurance cost into rate filings
  **provided** they write in wildfire-prone ZIP codes at ≥85% of their
  statewide market share (AS-DR2-042). Colorado's HB25-1182 obliges insurers to
  incorporate individual **and community-level** mitigation, including
  landscape-scale forest management, into pricing (AS-DR1-076).

The Colorado line matters: a statute already contemplates *community-scale*
mitigation changing a price. That is the prototype's whole mechanism, in law.

### Measured modelling of what regional mitigation is worth

- Moody's RMS with the American Forest Foundation: "21–59% of annual average
  losses to infrastructure due to fires could be avoided if fuel reduction
  projects were implemented"^81^ (modelled, not observed).
- Guy Carpenter with The Nature Conservancy, Paradise CA: a buffer plus updated
  building codes would cut value-at-risk "across most probabilities by 42%",
  "converting a one in 100-year loss level into a much rarer one in 350-year
  loss level", with USD 52M of average-annual-loss reduction^82^ (modelled).
- Against that: **RFF** found 22 of the top 25 P&C groups offer wildfire
  mitigation discounts, but "the discounts are relatively small", about **$100 a
  year** on average — roughly one tenth of the annualised cost of the work —
  and three insurers removed wildfire risk from rating altogether to avoid the
  discount requirement, affecting over 500,000 policies^84^.

  > **Correction (2026-10-05), applied after independent review.** The RFF
  > working paper is an analysis of **California** homeowners insurers under
  > California's mitigation-discount regime, not a national survey, and the
  > "$100 average" and the "one tenth of cost" figures are two separate
  > estimates that this evidence base previously presented as one arithmetic
  > comparison. The prototype no longer carries either number. The supported
  > statement, and the only one now on screen, is: *a study of leading
  > California homeowners insurers found widespread mitigation discounts, but
  > available credits generally cover only a small share of estimated retrofit
  > costs.* Do not reintroduce the figures, do not infer insurer motive from
  > them, and do not describe the finding as national.

**That $100-vs-cost gap is the market failure the prototype is about.** Nobody
can finance prevention out of an individual household's premium credit. Which
is exactly why the payer has to be plural.

## 3. Inferred — the pack's own reasoning, which we may argue but not assert

Each of these is marked in the pack as "This is our reasoning, not something we
found in use," or carries an `INFERRED` ID.

1. **Prevention could make some currently-uninsurable risk insurable again**,
   "mainly for single sites and geostationary satellites, since constellations
   rely on spares"^13^ (pack summary, stated as "We think").
2. **An insurer holding intervention-and-outcome logs would have the attribution
   evidence the market lacks.** K03-INF1: space weather "is rarely the
   attributable cause of claims" and operators "find in-orbit losses hard to
   prove"; storm-response logs "would give an insurer the attribution it lacks."
   The wildfire analogue is direct and is the prototype's moat argument.
3. **The durable edge is not the tool.** G3-X2 collects both sides: broker notes
   say early/proprietary data access persists (Travelers, Verisk, RMS), while
   expert calls say tool-based edges erode — "this moat eventually erodes
   because competitors can easily sign identical contracts with those same data
   providers" (Apollo.io, X2-06); and in mature lines "underwriting margins
   erode exceptionally fast because pricing models are tightly factored off
   shared claims data" (former Verisk VP, X2-05). G3-X2-07 is the synthesis:
   the durable cases rest on **pooled or proprietary data, enforceable IP, or
   the insurer's own loss history tuned into the model** — not on access.
4. **Someone other than the insurer usually already wants to pay.** The pack's
   "Seneca pattern", stated once per card: the payer is a utility, a
   municipality, an asset owner, a fire agency or a government, "for their own
   reasons", and the insurer benefits from lower losses and richer data.
5. **A regulator-mandated or operator-funded monitoring feed is the cheapest
   data an insurer will ever get.** G3-K08-INF: operators already collect it,
   "so an insurer that negotiates access would gain years of movement history…
   without buying sensors."

## 4. Concept — no company found; 2036 frame only

- Coordinated, cross-actor automatic response at regional scale. The pack finds
  the *pieces* deployed and finds **no integrated product**: K10's verdict is
  "Funded companies hold each piece, but we found none selling one integrated
  product," and K11's is "Companies sell each piece, but we found no insurer
  that triggers protective action."
- An insurer firing a client's physical protection from its own feed. K11:
  "An insurer that already sends warnings is one integration step from firing a
  client's barrier or asset-move plan from the same feed. **We found no insurer
  doing it.**"
- Exclusive or co-owned intervention-outcome data in a prevention partnership.
  Not found anywhere in 17 deals.
- Insurance capital financing a *wildfire* prevention network. The NCIUA bond is
  hurricane roofs; the pack is explicit that "That is home hardening, not storm
  modification," and the same caution applies here.

### Further corrections applied 2026-10-05

- **FORTIFIED / Hurricane Sally (55–74% fewer claims, 15–40% lower severity)**
  has been **removed from the prototype entirely**. It is a hurricane study,
  the peril mismatch distracted more than it informed, and the phrase "the
  best-measured prevention evidence anyone has" overstated a single study's
  standing. Screen 5 keeps only the strategic challenge, with no figures:
  *proven hardening and fuel management are the baseline; the network earns
  its place only where it adds measurable protection beyond them.*
- **Tahoe Donner** must be described as *an association policy whose quoted
  terms reflected **modelled** fuel-treatment benefits*. It is not a household
  renewal, not measured loss reduction, and not evidence of general market
  availability.
- **NCIUA** — the $600M is the **catastrophe bond's size**, not a prevention
  budget, and the resilience spread returns to mitigation only if losses stay
  below a set level. Both qualifications must appear wherever it is cited.
- **Exclusivity** is an absence of public evidence, not an established fact
  about the contracts. The only permitted wording is: *we found no public
  evidence of insurer-exclusive data rights in the deals we reviewed.*
- **Universal novelty claims are banned.** Not "no insurer is doing any of
  it", not "none of them sells the assembly". The permitted claim is: *we have
  not established a scaled regional example of this complete operating loop.*
- **Baloise** is *discontinued the programme without conclusive evidence of
  benefit*. Not "measurement is what failed" — that wording implies the
  intervention worked and only the instrument let it down, which the source
  does not support.

## 5. The eight facts worth putting on screen

Deliberately few. Everything else above is background for the argument.

1. **43 companies, twelve countries** already build the sensing, verification
   and suppression layers. (Pack Part 1 / G1.)
2. **Liberty Mutual and Tokio Marine** backed Pano AI's $44M Series B;
   **American Family and Mercury** backed BurnBot. Insurers are already paying
   for prevention. (^5,6,7^)
3. **Chubb, Travelers and Aviva Canada** already send crews to defend insured
   homes as fires approach. (^8,9,79^)
4. **Tahoe Donner**: an association policy whose quoted terms reflected
   *modelled* fuel-treatment benefits — the first its authors knew of to
   price community mitigation. Not a household renewal. (^82^)
5. **Aspen Fire Protection District** buys from Seneca, Pano AI and OroraTech —
   funded by foundation and donor money, not by one balance sheet. (^3,29^)
6. **NCIUA**: a **$600M catastrophe bond** — the bond's size, not a prevention
   budget — carrying a 0.35% resilience spread that funds FORTIFIED roofs
   only if losses stay below a set level. Hannover Re fronted it; a second
   bond repeats it. (^21,22,23^)
7. **Mitigation credits exist but are not sized to pay for mitigation.** A
   study of leading *California* homeowners insurers found widespread
   discounts covering only a small share of estimated retrofit costs. (^84^,
   as corrected above — the figures are no longer used.)
8. **Baloise stopped funding hail suppression in 2023** because the effect "has
   not been demonstrably measurable." (^28^)

Fact 8 is the one that earns the concept its honesty. It goes on screen 5, next
to the verification step, not buried in a footnote.

## 6. What the prototype must never claim

- Any Seneca performance figure. The pack records unresolved conflicts on
  Seneca's own payload (12 gallons vs ~20 gallons vs "more than 100 pounds" per
  aircraft; 500 vs 500–1,000 gallons of foam per five-drone sortie) and found
  **no source confirming delivery or a real-fire use**. Seneca's verified stage
  is: one announced five-year contract with delivery due summer 2026, plus field
  tests and demos.
- Any Kestrel Mutual premium, withdrawal, book size, loss ratio or ZIP-code
  decision. The pack contains none, and CLAUDE.md forbids presenting a
  fictional household as a real Kestrel Mutual policyholder.
- Any utility's wildfire liability figure attached to a named utility in our
  scenario.
- Any claim that the network *will* reduce losses. The measured numbers we have
  are modelled (RMS, Guy Carpenter) or from a different peril (FORTIFIED /
  Sally). Baloise is the counter-example and must stay visible.
- "Response in N minutes" as a capability claim. Our timeline is a scenario; the
  only verified detection-speed numbers in the pack belong to specific vendors
  in specific deployments (DigitalPath's 38% before 911; Satellites on Fire's
  claimed 35-minute lead over FIRMS) and are not ours to generalise.
